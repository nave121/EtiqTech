# Case 1 – Jotunheim metabolic condition Osmotic Pump Study (Request 90019)

## Summary
- Continuation study on mechanisms of Jotunheim metabolic condition using osmotic pump implantation and longitudinal monitoring.
- Involves surgery for pump placement, repeated glucose measurements, and follow-up over weeks.

## Main Reasons for Rejection
- **Analgesia dosing** – buprenorphine listed as a single 1 mg/kg IP dose after surgery, which is both too high and too infrequent relative to standard practice.
- **Post‑operative care** – no clear schedule for ongoing pain relief after implantation, despite a procedure categorized as pain grade 4.

## Ethical/Scientific Intent
- Study aims to model chronic Jotunheim metabolic condition progression and evaluate interventions in a controlled way.
- Committee expects that benefits (better understanding of disease course and treatment) are matched by robust peri‑operative welfare protections.

## Required Conceptual Fixes
- **Analgesia regimen**: use buprenorphine at standard doses (≈0.05–0.1 mg/kg SC) with explicit frequency (every 8–12 hours) and duration (48–72 hours post‑surgery).
- **Post‑op monitoring**: specify objective monitoring (weight, food intake, behavior) and thresholds for rescue analgesia or early euthanasia.
- **Form consistency**: ensure that all references to analgesia (drug tables and free text) describe the same, clinically reasonable regimen.
