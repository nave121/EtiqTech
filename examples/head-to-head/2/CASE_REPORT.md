# Case 2 – α‑Mimir-protein Aggregation Mouse Model (Request 90027)

## Summary
- Continuation of a pilot model investigating α‑Mimir-protein aggregation in a new mouse line.
- Includes stereotactic surgery, behavioral testing, imaging, and histology over a long follow‑up.

## Main Reasons for Rejection
- **Over‑scoped protocol** – includes many dependent sub‑studies (behavior, pathology, interventions) that all assume a successful new model, instead of first validating feasibility.
- **Incomplete virology details** – virus type (Adeno vs AAV), immunogenicity, and expected systemic effects are not clearly described, limiting welfare and biosafety evaluation.
- **Construct verification risk** – unclear whether the mutant α‑Mimir-protein is tagged or otherwise monitorable, raising the risk of a large animal use with uninterpretable negative results.
- **Anesthesia & methods gaps** – isoflurane anesthesia and behavioral protocols are not fully specified (concentrations, frequency, burden per animal).

## Ethical/Scientific Intent
- Aim is to create a robust mechanistic model of Svartalfheim disorderian pathology to support many downstream studies.
- To be ethical, the foundational feasibility step must be separated and well‑validated before exposing many animals to complex follow‑up protocols.

## Required Conceptual Fixes
- **Split into stages**: submit a focused feasibility protocol to establish the model; only if successful, follow with separate, narrower studies.
- **Clarify vector design**: explicitly document viral construct, tag, tropism, and expected impact on the animal.
- **Limit per‑animal burden**: cap the number of surgeries, behavioral tests, and imaging sessions per mouse and justify each in terms of scientific yield vs distress.
