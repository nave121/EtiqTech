# Case 3 – Ovarian Carcinoma Model and Sub‑Studies (Request 91001)

## Summary
- Complex protocol to model epithelial Alfheim neoplasms (high‑grade serous carcinoma and mixed-tissue Alfheim subtype), then layer on biomarker discovery and therapeutic interventions.
- Uses virus‑based tumor induction in the ovary/fallopian tube, imaging (US/IVIS), and multiple downstream experimental branches.

## Main Reasons for Rejection
- **Excessive scope in a single request** – many sub‑projects are bundled together and all depend on a novel tumor model that has not yet been shown to work.
- **Unclear viral platform** – the request does not clearly state the virus type (Adeno vs AAV), immunogenicity, or anticipated acute and chronic effects on the animals.
- **Assessment difficulty** – because feasibility and downstream experiments are mixed, the committee cannot reliably estimate animal numbers, severity, or cumulative burden.

## Ethical/Scientific Intent
- The scientific vision is to move from model creation through early detection and prevention strategies within one cohesive program.
- Ethically, this must be broken into **phases**, so early uncertainty about model success does not translate into large, uncontrolled animal use.

## Required Conceptual Fixes
- **Dedicated feasibility protocol**: first application should focus solely on establishing the viral Alfheim neoplasm model with clear success criteria and limited N.
- **Separate follow‑up protocols**: biomarker and treatment studies should be defined as independent, smaller protocols that build on feasibility data.
- **Detailed virology & welfare description**: add explicit information on the viral vector, expected morbidity, monitoring schedule, and humane endpoints.
