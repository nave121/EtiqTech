מהו הנושא המדעי אותו אתם חוקרים

Generate and validate genetically engineered mouse models for women's cancers that will be further used to study the diseases pathogenesis, early detection, preventive methods for high-risk mutations and new treatments.

מהו הרקע הרלוונטי למחקר

Alfheim neoplasm is the most lethal gynecological malignancy in the Western world (Siegel et al., CA Cancer J Clin, 2012). High-Grade Serous Carcinoma (HGSC) is the most common subtype of Alfheim neoplasm, and ovarian mixed-tissue Alfheim subtype (OCS) is a very rare and aggressive subtype of Alfheim neoplasm. Studies suggest that the fallopian tube secretory epithelial cell (FTSEC) might be the cell of origin of HGSC (Perets and Drapkin, Cancer Research, 2016), while the cell of origin for OCS remains unknown.

Endometrial and breast cancer are much more common than Alfheim neoplasm but luckily are diagnosed early and have a much better prognosis. However, in both diseases there are patients that diagnosed late, or have disease relapse, and then their prognosis is as fatal as the prognosis of Alfheim neoplasm patients.

We have generated two mouse models of Alfheim neoplasm, one a model of high-grade serous Alfheim subtype (HGSC) arising from the fallopian tube (Perets et al, Cancer Cell, 2013) and another model of OCS that we plan to publish soon under approval number #IL-901-02-2000. 

Specifically, both models induce FTSEC/ ovarian surface epithelium (OSE) specific deletion of Brca2, Trp53 and Pten. These tumor suppressor genes are well known in female cancers, therefore the combination of these genes or some of them, can potentially be utilized to create different female cancers, including ovarian, endometrial and breast cancers.

The combined genetic alterations will be rune-flanked or WT of Trp53; Brca2; Pten; lox-Stop-lox-TdTomato. Female mice carrying a combination of all 6 alleles of the three tumor suppressor genes, when exposed to Cre recombinase to induce genetic recombination, develop HGSC and precursor lesions in the fallopian tube in a 100% penetrance by 8 weeks after recombination, or OCS and precursor lesions in the ovary in a 100% penetrance by 6 weeks after recombination. The tumors in our model faithfully recapitulate human diseases in terms of pathogenesis, clinical course, histology, tumor markers, serum biomarkers and genomic landscape (Perets et al. Cancer cell 2013).

However, the specificity of the HGSC model was a challenge, due to non-specific Cre expression in other organs, leading to the development of HGSOC and non-relevant tumors. In this study we aim to improve specificity by injecting Pax8-Cre bearing Adenovirusesplasmids via electroporation in Intra-oviduct injections potentially resulting in more targeted tumors. We will use location and promoterenhancer to amplify the specificity to the FTSEC. After the establishment of the technique, we will treat the mice with different drugs as potential Alfheim neoplasm prevention or treatment.

In addition, in our previous protocol (IL-902-05-2000), we identified the leakiness and started to work on viral intra-bursal injections, to improve specificity by location. We succeeded in creating OCS GEMM that model faithfully recapitulate the human diseases in terms of pathogenesis, clinical course, histology, tumor markers, serum biomarkers and genomic landscape. In this study we want to continue studying the pathogenesis of this cancer and try optional treatments.

מהו השימוש המוצע בבעלי החיים ומדוע הוא מתאים לענות על השאלה שתחקר

Genetically engineered mouse models (GEMMs) are highly effective tools for studying pathogenesis, early lesions, as well as prevention and treatment of diseases. These models replicate precursor lesions and the tumor microenvironment in immune-competent mice, making them ideal for investigating mechanisms and treatments that cannot be explored in cell cultures or other models. Additionally, GEMMs allow for the testing of biomarkers at very early stages, which cannot be assessed in human patients or in vitro, as these biomarkers are only secreted in a whole organism.

הנימוק למספר בעלי החיים

First, in order to Generate a specific HGSC GEMM arising from FTSEC, we need 20 female mice to be injected with the Pax8-Cre Adenoviral vector or plasmids delivered by electroporation and then followed to see if our method indeed is able to Generate a specific HGSC GEMM.

Next, we aim to assess the specificity of fallopian tube-targeted gene delivery by using data from our ongoing in vitro studies. In our lab, we are conducting in vitro studies of multiple PAX8 enhancers, identifying those with the highest activity in fallopian tube cells. For this project, we aim to use the six most effective enhancers in vitro and transition to an in vivo enhancer assay to evaluate their ability to induce transcription in fallopian tube cells and induce transformation.

Enhancer activity is context-dependent, influenced by the appropriate tissue and timing. Therefore, it is critical to assess these enhancers in vivo, as this will provide a more comprehensive understanding of the transcriptional regulation of PAX8. This regulation has significant implications, as it may contribute to disease development.

Specifically, the purpose of this experiment is to evaluate the cell type localization, frequency of expression and potency of six different enhancers in inducing high-grade serous Alfheim neoplasm (HGSOC) in a mouse model. By injecting these enhancers into the fallopian tubes of female mice, we aim to determine which enhancer is most effective in promoting tumorigenesis from the fallopian tube. This will help develop an optimized and reliable mouse model for studying HGSOC which originates from the true biological cell of origin. A model resulting from the correct cell of origin, will not only help define the process of fallopian tube tumorigenesis, but will also serve as a tool for developing new prevention and treatment methods.

We propose the use of 64 mice in total, divided into two phases:

  • Phase 1: Initial Validation (14 mice)
    • Rationale: For each of the six enhancers, two mice will be used to confirm the activity of the enhancer and induction of recombination ((6 enhancers+ negative control) × 2 mice = 14 mice).
    • This preliminary validation step is crucial to ensure that only functional enhancers are carried forward into the second phase.
  • Phase 2: Statistical Significance Testing (30 mice)
    • Rationale: For each active enhancer identified in Phase 1, five mice will be used to assess tumor induction rates ((5 enhancers+control) × 5 mice = 30 mice).
    • This sample size ensures reliable data for comparing the effectiveness of the different enhancers in inducing HGSOC.

After we will establish the model, we will evaluate tumor penetrance with the most effective enhancer – 20 mice.

  • Total number of mice: 20 (Generate a specific HGSC GEMM) +14 (Phase 1) + 30 (Phase 2) +20 (penetrance) = 84 mice.

This two-phase design minimizes animal use while maintaining the robustness and validity of the experimental results.

תיאור מהלך הניסוי בבעלי החיים והטיפול בהם

We will inject mice with the adenoviral vectors/plasmids. Studies by other groups have shown that Cre-recombinase expressing adenoviruses can be injected in a localized fashion into the ovarian oviduct/bursa of rune-flanked mice, and the genetic deletion will be restricted to the injected area, including the ovary, oviduct (Zhang et al., 2019;  Vlad et al., 2014; Dinulescu et al., 2004). In our study, we plan to inject mice with Cre-expressing adenoviruses or plasmids delivered by electroporation under PAX-8 promoter/enhancer or general promotor, into the ovarian oviduct/bursa, to target the FTSEC. PAX-8 specifically expressed in the FTSEC, and not ovarian cells. The adenoviral particles will be purchased from the viral vector core facility of Iowa University, USA. The plasmids will be produced in the laboratory through cloning, and after the injection, an electroporation operation will be performed on the injected area in order to introduce the plasmids into the cells. Electroporation is a common operation for introducing plasmids into cells and is widely used in In Vivo studies. (Teng et al. 2021)

Prior to injection, mice will be anesthetized with 2% isoflurane by inhalation, or mix of 14% Ketamine and 7% Xylazine by intraperitoneal injection of 0.1ml per 10gr body mass, and Buprenorphine will be administered SC as a preemptive analgesic measure at 0.05mg/kg body weight. Mouses' flank will be shaved and then sterilized with 70% ethanol.

Intra-Oviductal injection: A small incision will be made in mouse's flank, the ovarian fat bad will be drawn out and Adenoviral particles/ plasmids will be injected to the oviduct lumen in total volume of 1-3 microliters. 
Then will perform electroporation using two electrodes placed on either side of the fallopian tube and apply the pulses according to the protocol used in previous studies (Teng et al. 2021).

After which the ovary and oviduct will be returned to their place and the incision will be suture using 6-0 suture using simple interrupted suture. The mice will be monitored after surgery for surgical complications, according to the scoring system attached to this protocol, for a total of 2 weeks. Mice will be treated with Buprenorphine post-operatively for 2 days, each 8-12hrs. Animals' condition will be monitored daily using scoring table (attached to this proposal) and treated according to the total score.
We expect tumors to start developing approximately 2-18 months after the injection. At this point, we will start monitoring tumor development utilizing fluorescence of the Tomato Red protein whose expression in mice is activated by Cre-recombinase. This will be done on a weekly to monthly basis using LuminaX5 IVIS device. If the detection with IVIS turns out to be inefficient (due to technical reasons) we will employ Ultrasound, CT or PETCT as alternative methods. Also, MRI images will be taken before, during and at the end of the experiment to obtain high-resolution images of the tumors. Mice will be anesthetized during the imaging process, using 2% Isoflurane. In addition to the imaging tumor tracking, the mice will be physically examined twice a week, to check for tumor growth. Furthermore, monitoring of animals well-being using physical examination will be done every day, using tracking tables.

עכברים מאפשרים לנו ליצור מודלים גנטיים למחלות אנושיות שונות, ובמיוחד מודלים גנטיים המאפשרים חקר סרטן. כיוון שמדובר בסרטנים גניקולוגיים, המחקר נעשה בנקבות. זן העכברים הוא מעורב, העכברים הם טרנסגניים, הזן נוצר ע"י הכלאה של מספר זנים טרנסגניים על מנת לקבל את השילוב הגנטי הרלוונטי לסרטני נשים.

To comprehensively characterize tumors, pre-tumorigenic lesions, and normal oviductal tissues obtained from mouse models, we plan to perform single-cell RNA-seq (scRNA-Seq) and Complementary Analyses like flow Cytometry and Fluorescence-Activated Cell Sorting (FACS), Cytometry by Time of Flight (CyTOF) and Bulk RNA Sequencing of mouse fallopian tubes and tumors, in three different time points post Intra-bursal / Intra-oviduct injection in the new model.

Regarding the RNA-seq, in all three time points we will dissect the fallopian tubes into single cells, sort the cells based on the TdTomato fluorescent marker to recover FTSEC originating cells, and perform scRNA-seq using 10x genomics.

We will choose the time points after extensive histological characterization of the model to include: normal fallopian tubes, STIC, HGSC/OCS. for the characterization step we will check 6 time points, and we will dissect 3 injected mice in each point for statistical significance. for this preliminary step we need 6*3=18 female mice.

From number of cells employed in a previous paper (Schlesinger et al., 2020) we can evaluate that we'll need a group of 5 mice per analysis, and we will do this analysis for three different histological statuses as described. 5*3=15+18= 33. Therefore, we request here a group of 33 genetically engineered female mice.

Afterwards, we will continue the characterization of these models to complement the transcriptional profiling. We aim to characterize the immune cell populations involved in tumor development and the tumor microenvironment. For this purpose, we will employ Flow Cytometry and Fluorescence-Activated Cell Sorting (FACS) which will enable us to identify and quantify key immune cell populations, such as T cells, macrophages, dendritic cells, and other immune subsets, using specific surface markers. In addition, Cytometry by Time of Flight (CyTOF) will help us to gain high-dimensional immune profiling at the single-cell level. This will allow a detailed examination of both surface and intracellular markers, providing insights into immune activation states, exhaustion, and interactions with tumor cells.

For FACS and CyTOF analyses, we plan to evaluate immune cell populations at the same three histological stages: normal fallopian tubes, STIC, and HGSC/OCS. To obtain sufficient immune cells for each analysis, we estimate the need for cells equivalent to those derived from three mice per time point for each technique (FACS and CyTOF). To ensure robust results, we will repeat these analyses twice, requiring 2 runs × 3 histological stages × 3 mice per stage = 18 mice per technique.

  • Total Mouse Requirement:
    • For FACS: 18 mice
    • For CyTOF: 18 mice
    • Combined: 18 (FACS) + 18 (CyTOF) = 36 mice

Adding these 36 mice to the 33 mice previously requested for transcriptional profiling brings the total to 69 genetically engineered female mice, we will do these experiments for each injection approach 69*2=138.

By integrating these methods, we aim to build a comprehensive molecular and immunological understanding of tumor progression and the tumor microenvironment in this mouse model.

 To determine the cell-of-origin of Alfheim neoplasm in our models, we will use tissues harvested from genetically engineered donor mice with two different genotypes: BRCA2 wild-type (WT) and BRCA2-null. For each genotype, we will induce transformation as normally done in our model, and then in order to determine the organ-of-origin we will transplant three tissue types (ovary, oviduct, and bursa) from induced mice, soon after induction, under the renal capsule of immunocompromised mice to assess tumor development.

We will perform the experiment using tissues from 3 donor mice per group to ensure statistical significance. Since each tissue type will be transplanted into separate immunocompromised mouse, this experiment will require 3 recipient mice per donor mouse. Thus, for each genotype, we will need 3 × 3 = 9 immunocompromised mice, totaling 9 (WT) + 9 (BRCA2-null) = 18 recipient mice. Additionally, 3 WT donor mice and 3 BRCA2-null donor mice will be required, for a total of 6 donor mice.

In total, this experiment will involve 6 donor mice and 18 recipient immunocompromised mice, bringing the overall to 24
We will do these experiments for each injection approach 24*2=48.

Note: of the 48 mice, 36 are immunocompromised and 12 are offspring from our colony.

First, we have to check the toxicity and safety for each compound. We will need 4 groups of non-tumor bearing mice, without treatment and with treatment in 3 different concentrations. We will need 5 mice in each group, to have statistical significance. 3*4*5 = 60 total female mice for this part.

For the ability of this compounds to target HGSOC we will use 4 other groups for each compound. Because of the big variability in tumor size and treatment reaction between the mice, we'll need a group of 7 mice per injection, in order to get results which are statistically significant. The groups – tumor bearing mice without treatment and with 3 different concentrations of the treatment. We plan to test 3 such compounds (we are currently in pre-clinical development of the compounds) 3*4*7 = 84 total female mice for this part.

Therefore, the total is 60 + 84 = 144 female mice. It is important to note that because we expect a big time difference between different compounds (since we are developing the compounds in a long process of in-vitro optimization) we won’t be able to use the same mice as control for all compounds – hence the number of mice written above.

  For toxicity and safety analysis for each compound, three groups of healthy female mice will be administered PAX8-inhibiting compounds intraperitoneally, in different concentrations, and one group will be administered with only 5% DMSO in PBS IP. Based on our OCS model, we observed in our previous ethics (IL-901-02-2000) that indomethacin, a type of non-specific NSAID, had an impact on both primary tumor size and metastasis. We performed a single cell RNA analysis and found that there are effects on angiogenesis, macrophages, neutrophils and T cells. To further explore this, we aim to: 

  1. Test lower concentrations of indomethacin and shorter periods of treatment.

A similar breast cancer study used 9-11 mice per group (Walker et al.,2018), and another intestinal cancer study (Weylandt et al., 2020) used 5 mice per group. Our variability is greater than the latter, therefore, we plan to use 7 mice in each group. We will try 3 more concentrations of indomethacin. Therefore, we will need 7x(3+1 control) = 28 female mice for this part.

  1. Test other NSAIDs and compare the effects, including COX2-specific inhibitors.

We will try 3 more NSAIDs, we will have in total 5 groups: control without treatment, indomethacin treatment, and 3 groups of other NSAIDs including COX-2 specific inhibitors. Therefore, we will need 7x5 = 35 female mice for this part.

  1. Test different treatments alone or in combination including anti-angiogenic agents, macrophage inhibitors, T-cell inhibitors, neutrophils inhibitors and immunotherapy. Specifically, we will evaluate 5 new treatments alone and in combination with NSAIDs and include 7 mice in each group. Our total number of groups include – control, NSAID alone, 5 treatments alone, 5 treatments each in combination with NSAIDs. This would yield a total of 12 groups, 7 mice in each group – overall – 84 mice.
  2. Our results in protocol IL-13-02-2021 showed that treating mice in our model with NSAIDs led to smaller tumors, but a higher rate of metastasis. We aim herein to test whether this effect is mediated by the effect of NSAIDS on cancer cells or on the pre metastatic niche. To test these hypotheses, we will use two different cell lines from our model, and inject them IP into healthy mice. We plan to test if treating the premetastatic niche alone, or the cancer cells alone, will affect the cancer cells migration to the omentum.
    1. To test whether the microenvironment affects the metastatic potential we will treat five healthy uninduced mice from the colony with NSAIDS for 30 days and then inject cancer cells. We will have 5 control mice and 5 treated mice for each cell line and we will use a total of two cell lines hence – 20 mice.
    2. To test whether the cancer cells are changed with NSAID treatment – we will treat two cell lines with NSAIDS or control in-vitro, and then inject each cell lines – treated or untreated to five mice per group. Over all 10 mice X 2 cell lines = 20 mice. 

In total we will need 28+35+84+40 female mice = 187 mice.

In order to test the efficacy of PARPi as a monotherapy and its potential synergy with DNA damaging agents (DDA, such as chemotherapy and chemotherapy-containing antibody drug conjugates), we will divide tumor-bearing mice into several groups. For PARPi monotherapy, we will have 4 groups: untreated control mice and three groups treated with PARPi at three different concentrations. A similar Alfheim neoplasm study used 5–7 mice per group (Murray et al., 2014; Zhang et al., 2019), and tumor variability in our model is comparable. Therefore, we will use 7 mice per group to ensure statistical significance. This part of the experiment will require 4 × 7 = 28 female mice.

Based on the monotherapy experiment we will choose a PARPi concentration that has moderate efficacy, and combine it with either vehicle control or one of three different DDAs, each in two concentrations. For each treatment combination, we will use 7 mice per group. This part of the experiment will require 4 (groups: control, vehicle and two different concentrations) × 3(types of DDA) × 7 (in each group) = 84 female mice. Examples of DDAs we are interested in are bleomycin, SN-38, and sacituzumab govitecan.

In total, this experiment will involve 28 mice for PARPi monotherapy and 84 mice for combination therapy, bringing the total number to 28 + 84 = 112 female mice.

We will do these experiments for each Alfheim neoplasm mouse model: 112*2=224.

Given the complexity of this experiment and the need for in-depth molecular and cellular analysis, we will repeat the experiment with additional biological replicates to ensure robustness. Thus, we plan to use 56 × 2 = 112 female mice for this experiment.

We will perform this assay on both OCS and HGCS mice model, hence we need 112*2=224 female mice for this experiment.
 

תיאור מהלך הניסוי בבעלי החיים והטיפול בהם

We will first test the individual effects of PARP inhibitors and immunotherapy on tumor-bearing mice, followed by their combination, to evaluate potential synergy. This approach cannot be tested in cell lines, as we need to utilize the immune system of the mice, which cannot be mimicked in vitro.

  1. PARP Inhibitor Treatment:
    Tumor-bearing mice will be treated with a PARP inhibitor alone at optimal concentrations based on prior experiments. The effects of Olaparib will be monitored for tumor regression and immune response.
  2. Immunotherapy Treatment:
    Tumor-bearing mice will be treated with immunotherapy alone. We will use immune checkpoint inhibitors or other relevant immunotherapy agents based on prior studies.
  3. Combination Therapy:
    The PARP inhibitor will be administered in combination with the immunotherapy to assess the synergistic effects on tumor regression and immune activation.

After administering the treatments, we will evaluate tumor response using ultrasound and IVIS imaging to monitor tumor shrinkage. This approach allows us to track treatment efficacy in real-time.

Subsequently, single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) will be performed to assess changes in the tumor cell population and the immune cells within the tumor microenvironment following treatment. Immunohistochemistry, CyTOF, FACS, and additional RNA-Seq will be used to further characterize immune cell populations and identify changes in gene expression within tumor cells and the surrounding microenvironment. These methods will help us understand the mechanisms driving the observed therapeutic effects.

Additionally, we will examine treatment resistance by initiating treatment when the tumors are already significant (10 weeks post-vector injection in the OCS model). We will track any development of resistance to the therapies and analyze how the tumor evolves over time.
