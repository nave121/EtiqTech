מהו הנושא המדעי אותו אתם חוקרים

Study ovarian mixed-tissue Alfheim subtype pathogenesis, early detection, preventive methods for high-risk mutations and new treatments.

מהו הרקע הרלוונטי למחקר

Ovarian mixed-tissue Alfheim subtype (OCS) is a rare and highly aggressive subtype of Alfheim neoplasm, characterized by both epithelial and mesenchymal components. Due to its rarity, limited clinical data are available, and there is an urgent need for relevant preclinical models to study its pathogenesis and develop effective therapies.

We have developed a novel genetically engineered mouse model (GEMM) of OCS, based on conditional deletion of tumor suppressor genes known to play critical roles in female cancers, including Trp53, Brca2, and Pten. These deletions are targeted to the ovarian surface epithelium (OSE) and/or fallopian tube secretory epithelial cells (FTSECs), using a Norns-loxP system under spatially controlled delivery. Specifically, female mice carrying combinations of rune-flanked alleles for the above genes (along with a Lox-Stop-Lox-TdTomato reporter) develop OCS with 100% penetrance within 6-9 weeks following Cre recombinase administration.

This OCS model faithfully recapitulates the human disease in terms of histopathology, clinical course, biomarker expression, and genomic features, and has been validated as a reliable tool for studying OCS biology and therapeutic response. The development of this model was previously partially described under protocol #IL-902-05-2000, and its continued use was approved under #IL-901-02-2000.

In the current study, we aim to further characterize the pathogenesis of OCS in this model and evaluate potential therapeutic approaches.

 

מהי השאלה הספציפית שתחקר בבקשה והרציונל המדעי לה

 

This proposal utilized offsprings of breeding colony (under the ethics approval #IL-158-12-24) for the following aims.

1. The pathogenesis of OCS including possible cells of origin and premalignant lesions.

2. Early and late biomarkers for early detection of OCS.

3. Possible preventive methods for genetic high-risk populations.

4. New treatment options for women's cancers of different female organs and subtypes and treatment effects.

מהו השימוש המוצע בבעלי החיים ומדוע הוא מתאים לענות על השאלה שתחקר

Genetically engineered mouse models (GEMMs) are highly effective tools for studying pathogenesis, early lesions, as well as prevention and treatment of diseases. These models replicate precursor lesions and the tumor microenvironment in immune-competent mice, making them ideal for investigating mechanisms and treatments that cannot be explored in cell cultures or other models. Additionally, GEMMs allow for the testing of biomarkers at very early stages, which cannot be assessed in human patients or in vitro, as these biomarkers are only secreted in a whole organism.

מהו התוצר החזוי של מחקר זה

Establishing the pathogenesis of female malignancies allowing to explore new possible prevention methods, targets for therapy and different novel therapies. This would allow us to study not only the utility of these therapies but also their bioavailability, pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics and toxicity profile.

הנימוק למספר בעלי החיים

So far we have created 2 primary and 2 ascites cell lines of Trp53-/-, Pten-/-, Brca2-/-. We will create one more primary and ascites cell lines of this genotype, and 3 more cell lines from 2 more genotypes of murine OCS tumors. 2 + 2x3 = 8 mice.

Next, we want to inject several cell lines into non-tumor-bearing mice, to study their ability to form a tumor, and their homing into specific organs, for example the omentum, which is a well-known homing site for Alfheim neoplasm. For this purpose, we will need 3 mice X 3 cell lines = 9 mice.

תיאור מהלך הניסוי בבעלי החיים והטיפול בהם

 

  1. For cell lines formation: female mice will be injected at the age of 8-12 weeks with adenoviral vectors to create OCS. The injections and work with viral vectors, including safety instructions, was previously done by us in the approved as described in ethics #IL-013-020-21.
    Injections description: Studies by other groups have shown that Cre-recombinase expressing adenoviruses can be injected in a localized fashion into the ovarian bursa of rune-flanked mice, and the genetic deletion will be restricted to the injected area, including the ovary, oviduct (Zhang et al., 2019;  Vlad et al., 2014; Dinulescu et al., 2004). In our study, we inject mice with 3ul of Cre-expressing adenoviruses under CMV promotor, into the ovarian bursa. The adenoviral particles will be purchased from the viral vector core facility of Iowa University, USA, the virus’s name: Ad5CMVCre-eGleam, the virus’s titer: 6x10^10pfu/ml, Adenovirus vectors prepared in the core are E1 and E3 deleted, these deletions are what make the vector replication deficient. According to the virus biosafety, in animal studies, adenoviral vectors require ABL2 containment.

Prior to injection, mice will be anesthetized with 2% isoflurane by inhalation, or mix of 14% Ketamine and 7% Xylazine by intraperitoneal injection of 0.1ml per 10gr body mass, and Buprenorphine will be administered SC as a preemptive analgesic measure at 0.05mg/kg body weight. Mouses' flank will be shaved and then sterilized with iodine, then 70% ethanol.

A small incision will be made in mouse's flank, the ovarian fat pad will be drawn out and Adenoviral particles will be injected in a total volume 3 microliters, using a 30G needle and the stripper pipettor.

After which the ovary and oviduct will be returned to their place, the peritoneal incision will be sutured using absorbable 6-0 suture using simple interrupted suture, and the skin with unabsorbable 6-0 suture. The mice will be monitored daily after surgery for surgical complications, for a total of 2 weeks. Mice will be treated with Buprenorphine post-operatively for 2 days, each 8-12hrs.
 

We expect tumors to start developing approximately 6 weeks after the injection. At this point, we will start monitoring tumor development utilizing fluorescence of the Tomato Red protein whose expression in mice is activated by Cre-recombinase. This will be done on a weekly to monthly basis using LuminaX5 IVIS device. If the detection with IVIS turns out to be inefficient (due to technical reasons) we will employ Ultrasound, CT or PETCT as alternative methods. Also, MRI images will be taken before, during and at the end of the experiment to obtain high-resolution images of the tumors. Mice will be anesthetized during the imaging process, using 2% Isoflurane. In addition to the imaging tumor tracking, the mice will be physically examined twice a week, to check for tumor growth and mice wellbeing.

 

After 10-20 weeks, when mice reach the humane end point, mice will be sacrificed with first isoflurane then CO2, and tumors will be dissected into single cells and continue grow as a cell line.

 

  1. For testing the cell lines ability to form tumors and cancer cells homing: mice will be intraperitoneally/subcutaneously injected with 1-10 million of each cell line. Tumor growth will be monitored via in vivo imaging system (IVIS) and ultrasound as described above. Mice will be sacrificed with first isoflurane then CO2 and tumors, including potential homing sites such as omentum and mesentery, will be collected and histologically evaluated.

In addition, cell lines will be used to further investigate in-vitro the tumorigenic qualities of the cancer cells, including migration and invasion.

האם מדובר במחקר הנובע מצורך רגולטורי לא
הנימוק למספר בעלי החיים

 

To determine the cell-of-origin of OCS in our model, we will use tissues harvested from genetically engineered donor mice with two different genotypes: BRCA2 wild-type (WT) and BRCA2-null. For each genotype, we will induce transformation as normally done in our model (see injection description in exp.1), and then in order to determine the organ-of-origin we will transplant three tissue types (ovary, oviduct, and bursa) from induced mice, one week after induction, under the renal capsule of immunocompromised mice to assess tumor development.

We will perform the experiment using tissues from 3 donor mice per group to ensure statistical significance. Since each tissue type will be transplanted into separate immunocompromised mouse, this experiment will require 3 recipient mice per donor mouse. Thus, for each genotype, we will need 3 × 3 = 9 immunocompromised mice, totaling 9 (WT) + 9 (BRCA2-null) = 18 recipient mice. Additionally, 3 WT donor mice and 3 BRCA2-null donor mice will be required, for a total of 6 donor mice.

In total, this experiment will involve 6 donor mice from our colony (ethics #158-12-24) and 18 recipient immunocompromised mice, bringing the overall to 24 mice.

תיאור מהלך הניסוי בבעלי החיים והטיפול בהם

Intra bursal injections of donor mice are as described in experiment 1. One week post donor injections, we will harvest the donor mice tissues pieces of the ovary, oviduct, and bursa. The harvested tissues will be transplanted under the renal capsule of immunocompromised mice. Each recipient mouse will receive one type of tissue (ovary, oviduct, or bursa). Tumor development will be monitored using ultrasound and in vivo imaging system (IVIS) once/two weeks until the end point.

Based on our OCS model, we observed in our previous ethics (IL-901-02-2000) that indomethacin, a type of non-specific NSAID, had an impact on both primary tumor size and metastasis. We performed a single cell RNA analysis and found that there are effects on angiogenesis, macrophages, neutrophils and T cells. To further explore this, we aim to:

  1. Test lower concentrations of indomethacin and shorter periods of treatment.

A similar breast cancer study used 9-11 mice per group (Walker et al.,2018), and another intestinal cancer study (Weylandt et al., 2020) used 5 mice per group. Our variability is greater than the latter, therefore, we plan to use 7 mice in each group. We will try 3 more concentrations of indomethacin. Therefore, we will need 7x(3+1 control) = 28 female mice for this part.

  1. Test other NSAIDs and compare the effects, including COX2-specific inhibitors.

We will try 3 more NSAIDs, we will have in total 5 groups: control without treatment, indomethacin treatment, and 3 groups of other NSAIDs including COX-2 specific inhibitors. Therefore, we will need 7x5 = 35 female mice for this part.

  1. Test different treatments alone or in combination including anti-angiogenic agents, macrophage inhibitors, T-cell inhibitors, neutrophils inhibitors and immunotherapy. Specifically, we will evaluate 5 new treatments alone and in combination with NSAIDs and include 7 mice in each group. Our total number of groups include – control, NSAID alone, 5 treatments alone, 5 treatments each in combination with NSAIDs. This would yield a total of 12 groups, 7 mice in each group – overall – 84 mice.
  2. Our results in protocol IL-13-02-2021 showed that treating mice in our model with NSAIDs led to smaller tumors, but a higher rate of metastasis. We aim herein to test whether this effect is mediated by the effect of NSAIDS on cancer cells or on the pre metastatic niche. To test these hypotheses, we will use two different cell lines from our model, and inject them IP into healthy mice. We plan to test if treating the premetastatic niche alone, or the cancer cells alone, will affect the cancer cells migration to the omentum.
    1. To test whether the microenvironment affects the metastatic potential we will treat five healthy uninduced mice from the colony with NSAIDS for 30 days and then inject cancer cells. We will have 5 control mice and 5 treated mice for each cell line and we will use a total of two cell lines hence – 20 mice.
    2. To test whether the cancer cells are changed with NSAID treatment – we will treat two cell lines with NSAIDS or control in-vitro, and then inject each cell lines – treated or untreated to five mice per group. Over all 10 mice X 2 cell lines = 20 mice.

 

In total we will need 28+35+84+40 female mice = 187 mice.

In order to test the efficacy of PARPi as a monotherapy and its potential synergy with DNA damaging agents (DDA, such as chemotherapy and chemotherapy-containing antibody drug conjugates), we will divide tumor-bearing mice into several groups. For PARPi monotherapy, we will have 4 groups: untreated control mice and three groups treated with PARPi at three different concentrations. A similar Alfheim neoplasm study used 5–7 mice per group (Murray et al., 2014; Zhang et al., 2019), and tumor variability in our model is comparable. Therefore, we will use 7 mice per group to ensure statistical significance. This part of the experiment will require 4 × 7 = 28 female mice.

Based on the monotherapy experiment we will choose a PARPi concentration that has moderate efficacy, and combine it with either vehicle control or one of three different DDAs, each in two concentrations. For each treatment combination, we will use 7 mice per group. This part of the experiment will require 4 (groups: control, vehicle and two different concentrations) × 3(types of DDA) × 7 (in each group) = 84 female mice. Examples of DDAs we are interested in are bleomycin, SN-38, and sacituzumab govitecan.

In total, this experiment will involve 28 mice for PARPi monotherapy and 84 mice for combination therapy, bringing the total number to 28 + 84 = 112 female mice.

 

תיאור מהלך הניסוי בבעלי החיים והטיפול בהם

Initially, we will test the synergy between PARPi and DDA in cell lines to evaluate the potential combination effects. If the results from the cell-based assays show promising synergy, we will proceed to in vivo studies with tumor-bearing mice.

First, female mice will be injected at the age of 8-12 weeks intra-bursa for tumor induction as previously described. In these studies, PARPi and/or DDA will be administered 3-5 days/week using intraperitoneal injections, starting when the tumor is 0.1-0.5cm3 volume (approximately 6-9 weeks post intra-bursal injection). The tumor volume will be evaluated via ultrasound and IVIS as previously described. Mice will be monitored for general health parameters, and any showing signs of pain or distress will be humanely euthanized with isoflurane followed by CO2.

Based on previous studies and the known variability of tumors in our genetically engineered mouse model, we will use 5 mice per treatment group to ensure statistical significance and account for biological heterogeneity. The experimental groups are as follows:

  1. PARP inhibitor monotherapy
  2. Immunotherapy monotherapy
  3. Combination therapy (PARP inhibitor + immunotherapy)
  4. Untreated control group

Since immune responses cannot be evaluated in vitro due to the absence of an intact immune system, in vivo experiments are essential. Each group will include 5 mice, resulting in 4 groups × 5 mice = 20 mice.

To allow for biological replicates and increase the robustness of downstream molecular and cellular analyses, we will repeat the treatment experiment once. Thus, the total number of mice required for the treatment phase is:
20 mice × 2 replicates = 40 mice

If therapeutic efficacy is observed, we will conduct an in-depth molecular and immunological characterization of the tumors to elucidate mechanisms of response, resistance, and changes in the tumor microenvironment:

 

We will perform scRNA-seq using the 10x Genomics platform to dissect transcriptional programs within tumor and immune cells at the single-cell level. This will provide insights into: cellular heterogeneity, tumor cell states, immune cell infiltration, pathways involved in response or resistance

We will sample tumors at three distinct time points:

  • Baseline (untreated)
  • Early response (before resistance)
  • Post-resistance

Based on prior literature (e.g., Schlesinger et al., 2020), 5 mice per time point are required to obtain sufficient numbers and variability of cells.
Total mice for scRNA-seq:
3 time points × 5 mice = 15 mice

To identify and quantify key immune cell populations, including T cells, macrophages, dendritic cells, and others, we will use multicolor flow cytometry and Fluorescence-Activated Cell Sorting (FACS). These analyses will help us to characterize immune infiltration dynamics, detect therapy-induced shifts in immune subsets and isolate specific cell populations for downstream validation

We will analyze 3 time points (as above), using 1 mouse per time point, repeated 3 times to ensure reproducibility.
Total mice for FACS:
3 time points × 1 mouse × 3 replicates = 9 mice

CyTOF will allow high-dimensional immune profiling at the single-cell level. This approach enables the detection of surface and intracellular markers simultaneously, providing information on immune activation states, exhaustion markers, functional phenotypes and tumor-immune interactions

We will analyze the same 3 time points with the same replicate design:
3 time points × 1 mouse × 3 replicates = 9 mice

This approach cannot be tested in cell lines, as we need to utilize the immune system of the mice, which cannot be mimicked in vitro.

PARP Inhibitor Treatment:
Tumor-bearing mice will be treated with a PARP inhibitor alone at optimal concentrations based on prior experiments. The effects of Olaparib will be monitored for tumor regression and immune response.

Immunotherapy Treatment:
Tumor-bearing mice will be treated with immunotherapy alone. We will use immune checkpoint inhibitors or other relevant immunotherapy agents based on prior studies.

Combination Therapy:
The PARP inhibitor will be administered in combination with the immunotherapy to assess the synergistic effects on tumor regression and immune activation.

After administering the treatments, we will evaluate tumor response using ultrasound and IVIS imaging to monitor tumor shrinkage. This approach allows us to track treatment efficacy in real-time.

Subsequently, single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) will be performed to assess changes in the tumor cell population and the immune cells within the tumor microenvironment following treatment. Immunohistochemistry, CyTOF, FACS, and additional RNA-Seq will be used to further characterize immune cell populations and identify changes in gene expression within tumor cells and the surrounding microenvironment. These methods will help us understand the mechanisms driving the observed therapeutic effects.

Additionally, we will examine treatment resistance by initiating treatment when the tumors are already significant (10 weeks post-vector injection). We will track any development of resistance to the therapies and analyze how the tumor evolves over time.
