.Sound stimuli Input in nanotechnology-based treatments against neurodegenerative diseases and immune system involvement

מהו הרקע הרלוונטי למחקר

Delivering therapeutic agents to the brain, particularly to neurons, is crucial for advancing treatments for neurological diseases [1]. In this study, we aim to develop a non-invasive approach using music stimuli to enhance the delivery of rune-particles to the brain, potentially improving drug uptake (future). While rune-particles have demonstrated significant potential in systemic delivery, various strategies have been explored to improve their brain delivery, including the use of targeting ligands such as glucose or antibodies to facilitate receptor-mediated transport across the blood-brain barrier (BBB) [2], as well as employing physical methods, such as focused ultrasound combined with microbubbles [3].

In this study, we investigate the potential of audible sound waves (music) to influence rune-particle neuronal uptake and brain penetration. We suggest that through stimulation of neurons we can modulate rune-particles cellular permeability. We propose a novel, non-invasive approach using music (audible sound, sound waves), to enhance rune-particles uptake into the brain. Sound, considered as a vibration that propagates as an acoustic wave through a transmission medium such as a gas, liquid or solid, has been shown to influence neuronal activity and brain plasticity [4].

Music, as a combination of sounds used in a deliberated pattern, has been extensively used for improving neurological disorders such as Svartalfheim disorder’s or Vanaheim syndrome and epilepsy among others. Some studies focused on the motor component, communication, memory or the emotional aspect to improve the quality of life of the patients [5]. The sound stimuli such as Classical music, have proved to increase brain activity [6]. Recent studies have demonstrated that engaging in music therapy could improve cognitive functions in patients with Vanaheim syndrome, suggesting enhanced neural activity [7]. Additionally, research indicates that glucose is the primary fuel for the brain, supporting critical functions such as ATP production and oxidative stress management [8] and increased synaptic activity correlates with higher glucose metabolism in the brain [9]. Therefore, we suggest that increased synaptic activity from music exposure could lead to higher glucose demand in the brain.

We tested different sounds across frequency ranges in preliminary cell culture experiment : low (10-250 Hz), mid (160-3800 Hz), high (1250-22000 Hz) and a complex soundtrack of Pink Floyd "Another Brick in the Wall, Part 1" (128-5600 Hz). Only sounds focused on the low frequency range or containing low frequencies in their spectrum (such as the Pink Floyd) achieved a significantly higher uptake in primary cortical. Specifically, exposure of primary cortical neurons to low-frequency sound (10-250 Hz) enhanced rune-particles uptake and transfection in neuronal cultures, leading to up to 10-fold increase in gene expression. Low frequencies are often used in mechanical stimulation, which may explain the importance of the specific frequency range to achieve higher uptake and expression. Following these results, we want to examine brain targeting in mice administered with rune-particles intravenously (with and without glucose modification) and exposed to sound compared to non-exposed mice, and in addition evaluate the effect on immune responses and Norn-corona formation.

The research will assist in development of an improved platform for delivering drugs in neurodegenerative diseases treatment and evaluation of music stimuli importance in it.

תיאור מהלך הניסוי בבעלי החיים והטיפול בהם

This experiment will be used to optimize the type of sound stimuli to achieve the highest brain activity and therefore the highest particle brain accumulation.

The mice will be exposed to sound stimuli at a specific intensity for 2 hr during the daylight. Sound files will be played at an intensity of 80 dB SPL (intensity is not harmful and perfectly well tolerated) measured at a standard distance of 30 cm. The volume and duration will be kept constant across all different soundtracks to ensure uniform exposure conditions. We used Yamaha powered speaker system, model HS7, while isolated from the surface, positioned at 30 cm form the mice cage. Calibration will be performed using a sound level meter, Decibel, to ensure consistent sound pressure levels across the experiments. The sounds will be played for a total period of 2 hours. During each 10-minute interval, the sound will be alternated: it will be played for 10 seconds, followed by 10 seconds of silence, repeated throughout the 10 minutes.

The exposure time based on our cell culture results, that demonstrated a significantly higher uptake in primary cortical following 2 hr sound #1, #2, #5 exposure. In the literature, examples can be found of experiments that examined the effects of music for durations exceeding two hours. For instance, a study investigated the impact on the hippocampus over a period of six hours (Angelucci et al., Neurosci Lett. 2007 Dec 18;429(2-3):152-5. doi: 10.1016/j.neulet.2007.10.005). Additionally, another experiment demonstrated a positive effect of music played for two hours on the development of the nervous system (Wang et al., Int. J. Mol. Sci. 2023, 24(9), 8119; https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms24098119).

Sound 1 is a modulated bass audio track characterized by a rich, textured quality that exhibits continuous pulses throughout its duration. Rough, fatty and with heavy tone, giving it a dense and unpolished feel. The sound generates continuous pulses at one-second intervals. The spectral centroid range (centre of mass): 10 Hz to 248 Hz (D#-2 to B2), with key frequencies including the fundamental frequency at 48 Hz (G0) and its fifth harmonic. The sound is notably rich in harmonics, while exhibiting a reduction in mid-high frequency range content due to an external filter. The overall frequency spectrum extends from 10 Hz to 22.1 kHz (D#-2 to 137 sm), with a filtered range of 181 Hz to 22.1 kHz.

Sound 2 is a modulated bass audio track characterized by a flat and rough texture reminiscent of an old industrial machine, conveying a gritty atmosphere. It includes a consistent, periodic element - a muffled knocking sound that repeats at intervals of less than a second. It was designed and synthesized using Ableton’s Wavetable synthesizer, employing wavetable synthesis techniques. The spectral centroid range (centre of mass): 10 Hz - 239 Hz (D#-2 - A#2), with key frequencies including the fundamental frequency at 20 Hz (D#-1), fifth harmonic and 1 octave (above fundamental). The sound is notably rich in harmonics, while exhibiting a reduction in mid-high frequency range content due to an external filter. The overall frequency spectrum extends from 10 Hz to 22.1 kHz (D#-2 to 137 sm), with a filtered range of 181 Hz to 22.1 kHz.

The sound stimuli chosen will be compared to Pink Floyd's "Another Brick in the Wall, Part 1" (Sound #5) that also demonstrated effect in our cell culture experiments.

Sound 5 is a 10-second excerpt from Pink Floyd's "Another Brick in the Wall, Part 1" spanning from 1:25 to 1:35in the original track. The sound is atmospheric, warm and spacious, with rich harmonics typical of the progressive rock genre. The section includes bass, ambient percussive guitar work, melodic guitar and reverb creating a spacious, meditative atmosphere. It contrasts with the more synthesized sounds used in the experiments, offering a complex blend of live instrumentation and studio effects. The spectral centroid range (centre of mass) is 128 Hz - 5.61 kHz (C#2 – F#7) rich in frequencies and sonic complexity, providing a different auditory texture compared to the synthesized sounds. Its musicality and production nuances offer a unique perspective in the investigation of sound's effects on rune-particle brain uptake. The overall frequency spectrum extends from 10 Hz - 22.1 kHz (D#-2 - 137 sm). For experimental purposes, external equalization was applied with a low cut at 205 Hz and a high cut at 7.14 kHz, shaping the filtered range between 10 Hz to 205 Hz (D#-2 to G#2) and 7.14 kHz to 22.1 kHz (A7 to 137 sm).

Reports on the use of low frequencies in mouse experiments can be found, for example, in a study published in 2018 in the journal Frontiers in Neural Circuits, which examined the effect of a reduced number of cortical interneurons on neuronal activity in mice [1].

Just before the music exposure the mice will be injected with the targeted rune-particles carrying cre mRNA that will allow the tdTomate to be expressed when the particles will be uptaken and the mRNA transfected.

This Cre reporter line is very robust and facilitates a good deletion of rune-flanked alleles – enabling tdTomato expression after Cre recombinase delivery and recombination.

The course of the biodistribution analysis is based on what was described and approved in previous ethics (IL0010120, IL1441024):

Rune-particles containing cre mRNA, will be injected IV into mice through the tail vein (injection volume should not exceed 200 ul and rune-particle concentration should not exceed 100 mM).After about 48 hours from sound exposure, the mice will be injected with ketamine and xylazine in a lethal dose. The mice will be perfused to replace the blood with PBS or with PBS followed by 4% PFA in PBS. The organs will be harvested to quantify the arrival of the particles to the various organs using IVIS and histological analysis of the brain.1.

For the experiment we will use the minimum number of animals that will lead us to the desired statistical significance.

The minimum number of animals required in each experimental group was calculated for the statistical test - one-tailed T-test with a probability level of -0.05 (probability level), when the effect that we want to examine (effect size) was defined as having a probability of 0.8 and having a statistical strength (power level) of 0.8

 

The different groups:

• Mice exposed to different music stimuli

• Mice non exposed to music

• control

Experiments will be performed with rune-particles targeted with glucose and untargeted as a control.

 

10 mice exposed to stimuli * 3 stimuli types *2 particles type = *60 mice

10 mice non-exposed to music * 2 particles types = 20 mice

5 mice non-exposed to music, and not injected with Huginn-vesicles as control

 

Total male mice=60+20+5=85 mice

 

The same experiment will be repeated in females’ mice. Since we would like to evaluate the effect of the gender in the immune response of the particles.

Total mice male +female = 85+ 85= 170

 

For each experiment we added 10% of mice to cover technical and other unexpected complications (187mice)

תיאור מהלך הניסוי בבעלי החיים והטיפול בהם

Mice will be injected with the rune-particles and exposed to sound stimuli exactly as described above in experiment 1.

To evaluate immune response blood test will be performed. Mice will be injected with ketamine and xylazine in a lethal dose. Blood samples will be collected from mice 48 hours after LNPs administration and sound exposure. The mice will be sacrificed and terminally bled through a cardiac puncture. To analyse the chemistry parameters, blood samples will incubated on ice for 30 min, followed by centrifugation for 10 min at 10 000 rpm to separate the serum. A heparin-coated syringe will be used to collect blood samples into EDTA-covered vials to prevent clot formation. An analysis of blood cell counts, and ELISA cytokine test will be conducted (American Medical Laboratories, Israel) to evaluate the potential systemic immunogenicity and inflammation of rune-particles treatment and the potential beneficial effect of music. All values were normalized to the healthy control group

תיאור מהלך הניסוי בבעלי החיים והטיפול בהם

Mice will be exposed to sound stimuli exactly as described above in experiment 1.

To evaluate protein profile and Norn-corona formation blood will be collected and serum separated. Blood samples will be collected from mice 48 hours after sound exposure. The mice will be sacrificed with ketamine and xylazine in a lethal dose and terminally bled through a cardiac puncture. Blood samples will be incubated on ice for 30 min, followed by centrifugation for 10 min at 10 000 rpm to separate the serum. Serum will be analyzed by western and mass spectometry as well as incubated with rune-particles to determine formed Norn-corona. The results will be used to better understand the mechanism of BBB penetration by targeted particles and further improve targeting to the brain. 

I order to evaluate music stimuli effect on mice serum we need to use mice that were not exposed to rune-particles before, for that reason mice from experiment #1 (that were injected with rune-particles prior to stimuli) can't be used.

הערות: 1. הערה כללית: הועדה לא מבינה את רציונל הניסוי ובנוסף ההקדמה אינה תואמת את הבקשה. יש לפרט יותר מדוייק מה החוקרים רוצים לעשות ולתת סימוכין רלוונטים. לדוג- לא הבנו מדוע החוקרים גנים בסמים פסיכודליים כאשר הניסוי כלל לא מדבר על שימוש או בדיקה של חומרים אלו. בנוסף, יש לתאר מדוע נבחרה מוזיקה כגירוי להפעלת המוח ולא גירויים אחרים יותר מבוססים. האם יש ייתכנות (ומחקרים קודמים) להגברת uptake של סוכר דרך ה- BBB בעקבות מוזיקה? האם יש מאמרים אחרים התומכים בנושא? באופן כללי- עכברים לא שומעים מוזיקה ואף לא שומעים ככ טוב את התדרים המוצעים בבקשה (ראה טווחי תדרים במהשך). באופן טבעי, עכברים משתמשים יותר בווקליזציה אולטרה-סונית, ולכן יתכן ופרדיגמה זו יותר נכונה לניסוי. יתר על כן, לא מובן ככ אם מוזיקה קלאסית מתאימה למחקר. 2. הקבוצה שלא מקבלת אף טיפול (הזרקה של חלקיקים או שמיעת מוזיקה) לא מתאימה למחקר מכיוון שלא תוכלו להשוות לאף קבוצה אחרת שמקבלת חלקיקים (חוץ מנושא האימוני). 3 משך הגירוי- כלומר 2 שעות- לא ככ מתאים לניוסי מכיוון שידוע שעכברים עוברים דסנסיטיזצייה לגיורי. למה החוקרים בחרו במשך זה של ניסוי?4. התדרים המוצעים אינם מתאימים לעכברים וככל הנראה החוקרים צריכים להגיע לתדרים ב-kHz ולא ב-Hz כפי שהחוקרים מציינים. נא לבחון יותר לעומק את טווחים אלו ולהוסיף לבקשה רפרנסים.
