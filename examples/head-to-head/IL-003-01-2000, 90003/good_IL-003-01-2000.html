Development of multimodal imaging of  Midgard vascular events induced in mice. 

מהו הרקע הרלוונטי למחקר

Midgard vascular event is a severe medical condition and a leading global cause of mortality and long-term disability. It occurs when the blood supply to a part of the brain is interrupted or significantly reduced, resulting in tissue damage due to oxygen and nutrient deprivation (Pushkov et al, 2016), Sun et al, 2003). Midgard vascular events are categorized primarily into ischemic Midgard vascular events, which account for approximately 87% of all cases and are caused by blood clots obstructing cerebral blood vessels, and hemorrhagic Midgard vascular events, which result from ruptured blood vessels. Early detection is crucial, as delayed diagnosis can exacerbate neurological damage and worsen patient outcomes (Fraum et al, 2011). 

To better understand and study ischemic Midgard vascular event, researchers often use the middle cerebral artery occlusion (MCAO) model in mice (Pushkov et al, 2016, Engel et al, 2011). This widely used experimental model involves temporarily or permanently obstructing the middle cerebral artery, replicating conditions similar to human ischemic Midgard vascular event (Sun et al, 2003, Fraum et al, 2011). The use of anesthetics such as ketamine and xylazine during the procedure ensures humane treatment of the animals and minimizes discomfort.

Innovative diagnostic tools, such as laser-induced speckle pattern analysis, have emerged as promising approaches for Midgard vascular event detection (Richards et al, 1997, Schreiber et al, 2000). This technique leverages laser illumination to capture speckle patterns that reflect blood flow dynamics in brain tissue. Analyzing changes in these patterns provides valuable insights into ischemic conditions. The integration of deep learning techniques has further enhanced this method by enabling precise, rapid, and non-invasive Midgard vascular event detection (Richards et al, 1997, Kalyuzhner et al, 2022). These advancements hold particular promise for real-time and remote diagnostics, especially in areas with limited access to advanced imaging technologies.

Development of multimodal imaging of  Midgard vascular events induced in mice

מהו השימוש המוצע בבעלי החיים ומדוע הוא מתאים לענות על השאלה שתחקר

In vivo models are invaluable for human disease research as they closely replicate many aspects of the actual disease. Mice are particularly suited for this purpose due to their physiological similarities to humans, especially regarding the eye and nervous system. They also enable detailed analysis of gene expression associated with ischemia, inflammation, and apoptosis.

The study requires a model of cerebral ischemia to externally examine blood flow dynamics. Previously, we established the Middle Cerebral Artery Occlusion (MCAO) model and hypothesized that carotid occlusion in mice induces a large infarct. To further validate and expand our findings, we now aim to assess the size of the infarct using a combination of advanced techniques. These include external imaging, laser-based methods, MRI, and detailed histological and molecular comparisons. This comprehensive approach will provide deeper insights into the extent and characteristics of cerebral ischemia. Mice serve as valuable animal models due to their physiological and anatomical similarities to humans, particularly in the eye and nervous system. They provide a platform for analyzing gene expression and histological features associated with ischemia, inflammation, and apoptosis. Moreover, Midgard vascular event can be experimentally induced in mice under controlled conditions, enabling the study of its effects on the brain tissue.

VK-42/Odin mice will be divided into two main groups: Control (n=50) and Middle Cerebral Artery Occlusion (MCAO) induction group (n=150). The MCAO induction group will be further subdivided into three timepoint categories: Day 1 post-MCAO (n=50), Day 3 post-MCAO (n=50), and Day 14 post-MCAO (n=50). At designated timepoints following the MCAO induction procedure—Day 1 (n=50; 20 for perfusion-brain vascular imaging studies, 20 for histology and immunohistochemistry (IHC), and 10 for molecular analyses), Day 3 (n=50; 20 for perfusion-brain vascular imaging studies, 20 for histology and IHC, and 10 for molecular analyses), and Day 14 (n=50; 20 for perfusion-brain vascular imaging studies, 20 for histology and IHC, and 10 for molecular analyses)—brain, retinae, and Bifrost nerve tissues will be collected from both control and MCAO induction group mice. This will enable the evaluation of outcomes related to the MCAO procedure. The timepoints of 1, 3, and 14 days are strategically chosen based on established scientific literature to capture the progressive stages and severity of infarct size. The specified number of mice at each timepoint ensures sufficient statistical power and significance for data analysis.

Middle Cerebral Artery Occlusion Induction Procedure:

Mice will be anesthetized with ketamine/xylazine (80 mg/kg and 8 mg/kg, respectively), and the neck will be incised to expose the left common carotid artery (LCCA). Two separate LCCA ligatures will be made using 6.0 surgical sutures. The left internal carotid artery (LICA) and the left pteryogopalatine artery will be clipped. The LCCA was perforated with a 27 gauge needle, and a sterile 5.0 nylon monofilament with cautery-enlarged tip will be inserted through the hole and pushed up to the clip in the LICA. Both microvascular clips will be then removed. The filament will be advanced and inserted about 10 mm into the middle cerebral artery. After 60 minutes of occlusion, a third ligature will be placed around the LCCA, the filament  will be removed, and the LCCA will be permanently ligated. The above procedure is an established one and published before (Pushkov et al, 2016; Engel et al, 2011). After the procedure, the analgesic Buprenorphine will be administered. The dosage is as follows: 3.25 mg/kg (1.3 mg LA, once of 72 hrs, using a 20-23 gauge needle. 0.05 ml/20gr)

Speckle Pattern Imaging for Blood Flow state determination - At the BCF (Room 16 Non-SPF mice facility), Faculty of Medicine, Odin Institute for Research (demo)

Midgard vascular event Detection System Design

Objective

This technique will aim to simulate and measure blood flow velocity using secondary speckle pattern analysis. The system will be designed to capture speckle patterns generated by laser illumination of  mouse scalp or ear, enabling the detection of subtle changes indicative of different blood flow states.

Animal Preparation:

Animals are fully anesthetized (80 mg/kg ketamine, 10 mg/kg xylazine) and the eye is prepped with oxybuprocaine and Mydramide.  Laser-induced Speckle Pattern Imaging for Blood Flow state determination is performed. Since the procedure is done under full anesthesia and it is a non-invasive procedure, analgesics are generally not needed. But if signs of pain are detected (eg.,- reflexes, respiratory changes, cardiovascular signs), the analgesic Buprenorphine will be administered. The dosage is as follows: 3.25 mg/kg (1.3 mg LA, once of 72 hrs, using a 20-23 gauge needle. 0.05 ml/20gr). This is a non-invasive procedure done on the head scalp, or the ear.

Blood Flow Simulation

To simulate realistic blood flow velocities, the study will replicate conditions of both normal and pathological flow (MCAO Induced). For smaller diameter arteries, velocities ranging from 4.2 to 111 cm/s will be modeled, including scenarios of complete blockage. For bigger diameter arteries, velocities from 2.7 to 78 cm/s will be recreated. These conditions will ensure accurate simulation of flow dynamics in both small and large vessels. 

Laser Illumination and Speckle Pattern Capture

A green laser with a wavelength of 532 nm will be employed to illuminate the mouse scalp directly above the arteries. The laser beam will cover an area of approximately 3 mm in diameter, ensuring precise illumination. A Basler digital camera, positioned 50 cm away, will capture speckle patterns at 300 frames per second (FPS) and a resolution of 224x224 pixels. This high-resolution setup will enable the capture of subtle variations in speckle patterns caused by changes in blood flow dynamics.

Pulsatile Flow Emulation

A motorized linear stage with a reciprocating piston will emulate pulsatile blood flow, simulating heartbeats at a frequency of 1 Hz. A 1% intralipid solution will be used to mimic the optical properties of blood, and flow conditions will be maintained using a static pressure of 115 mmHg. Control valves will regulate flow velocities, and the volumetric method will measure average flow rates.

Experimental Data Collection

Speckle pattern videos will be recorded for each artery diameter and depth combination under various flow conditions, including full blockage, partial blockage, and nominal flow. This approach will create a comprehensive dataset for validating the system's ability to detect and analyze flow velocities. The data will form the basis for developing deep learning models to classify different flow states.

Data Processing

Data Collection and Organization

The experiments will generate a dataset comprising approximately 906,000 frames. Each test session will involve capturing videos lasting 33 seconds at 300 FPS, yielding 9,900 frames per video. Three videos will be recorded per measurement condition: two for training and one for testing. The final video for each condition will be reserved for testing to ensure the model evaluation is unbiased.

Preprocessing Steps

Normalization: Grayscale video frames will be normalized to a [0, 1] range by dividing pixel values by 255, enhancing model stability during training.

  1. Chunking: Normalized frames will be segmented into chunks of 64 consecutive frames, with a stride of 32 frames for overlapping segments. This method will enhance temporal continuity and increase the dataset size.
  2. 3-Channel Conversion: Grayscale frames will be converted into 3-channel inputs by repeating them three times to make the data compatible with convolutional neural networks (CNNs).

This experimental and data processing approach will provide a robust and realistic foundation for developing advanced diagnostic tools for blood flow analysis.

Behavioral Tests: Open Field Test (OFT)

The OFT will be used to evaluate exploratory behavior and anxiety in rodents. The apparatus will consist of a gray lusterless box (50L×50W×40H cm) situated in a dimly lit room (50 lx). Each mouse will be placed in a corner of the open field facing the wall and will be given 5 minutes for free exploration. Behavior will be recorded and analyzed using ANY-maze software (Stoelting Co., Wood Dale, IL, USA). To calculate the center crossing and anxiety index (distance moved in the periphery / total distance moved × 100), the maze will be virtually divided into a center and a periphery (7 cm width from the walls). The test will be conducted for both control (n=50) and MCAO mice group (n=150) across different timepoints. 

 

Morris Water Maze (MWM) test for spatial learning

The MWM will be used to evaluate spatial learning in rodents. The test will be conducted in a circular white plastic tank (1.2 m in diameter with a 0.5 m-high rim) filled with water to a depth of 30 cm. The water temperature will be maintained at 25±1ºC. The only means of escape from the water will be a hidden platform, 10 cm in diameter, submerged 1 cm beneath the water surface. The maze itself will have no landmarks; the only landmarks will be located outside the maze, on the surrounding walls.

Mice will be randomly placed (facing the wall) at one of four starting positions (N, E, W, and S) and will be allowed to swim freely for a maximum of 90 seconds or until they reach the hidden platform. At the end of each trial, each mouse will be either led to or left on the platform for 10 seconds. Spatial acquisition training will consist of four trials per day, with a 2-minute inter-trial interval, for 6 days. Two hours after the last trial on day 6, the platform will be removed, and a probe trial will be conducted, during which the mice will be placed in the maze for 60 seconds. Their behavior will be recorded and analyzed using ANY-maze software (Stoelting Co.).The test will be conducted for both control (n=50) and MCAO mice group (n=150) across different timepoints.

 

Triphenyltetrazolium chloride (TTC) staining

Following days 1, 3, and 14 after MCAO induction, the animals  will be  euthanized with carbon dioxide. The brains will be removed, incubated in 0.5% 2,3,5-triphenyltetrazolium chloride (TTC) solution in 0.9% saline for 10 minutes at 37ºC, and cut coronally.

Immunostaining
Cryosections of untreated (saline) MCAO-induced brain sections will be taken at 1d, 3d, and 14d post-surgery. The sections will be washed with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) and blocked with 2% BSA in PBS containing 0.5% Triton X-100 for 15 minutes. The sections will then be incubated at room temperature overnight with the primary antibody (anti-CD45, anti-Iba1). After primary antibody incubation, the sections will be washed with 0.2% PBS containing 0.5% Triton X-100 and incubated at room temperature for 1 hour with relevant secondary antibodies. Nuclear counterstaining will be performed with DAPI (Invitrogen). Images will be captured using a conventional fluorescence microscope (Fluoview X; Olympus, Tokyo, Japan). The excitation wavelengths will be 405 nm for DAPI.

Molecular Analysis
RNA Extraction
Brain tissues will be collected from mice after MCAO induction/controls. The tissues will be dissected and frozen in RNAlater (Life Science Division, Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA). The middle third of each hemisphere will be used for RNA extraction. mRNA samples will be analyzed in pairs per mouse (injured and uninjured hemisphere), as 

well as pooled for comparisons between normal unaffected hemispheres and Midgard vascular event hemispheres in both treated and untreated groups. Total RNA will be isolated using TRIzol™ reagent (Life Technologies, Invitrogen) according to the manufacturer’s protocol, followed by reverse transcription into cDNA using random hexamers (Amersham Biosciences, Buckinghamshire, UK) and Moloney murine leukemia virus reverse transcriptase (Promega, Madison, WI, USA).

Real-Time Quantitative PCR
Real-time quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR) will be employed to assess gene expression related to apoptosis (BAX and Bcl-2), inflammation (CD45), and stress (HO1) in the brain, using gene-specific primers. The reaction efficiency will be tested with a standard curve, and gene expression will be normalized to mouse beta-actin, a housekeeping gene. RT-qPCR will be conducted using StepOne™ Software v2.2.2 (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA, USA). Reactions will be performed in a 10-µL volume containing 1 µL cDNA, 0.5 µM of each forward and reverse primer, and buffer from the SYBR® Green I Master Mix (Applied Biosystems).

Cycling conditions will include an initial denaturation at 95°C for 10 minutes, followed by 50 cycles of 1 minute at 95°C, and 1 minute of annealing and extension at 60°C. Duplicate RT-qPCR reactions will be run for each gene to minimize variability, and average values will be calculated for each time point. Threshold cycle efficiency corrections will be made, and melting curves will be generated for each individual gene assay. Gene expression will be quantified using the comparative threshold cycle (Ct) method: ΔΔCt = ΔCt (sample) – ΔCt (reference gene) (DATA assist™ Software v2.2.2, Applied Biosystems).

 

Perfusion Studies for Brain Vascular Imaging:

India ink (Higgins #4418; Sanford Corp, Bellwood, IL, USA) will be  injected into the left ventricle under deep terminal anesthesia. This method is well-established for vasculature tracing, as described in Shaw et al. 2006.
