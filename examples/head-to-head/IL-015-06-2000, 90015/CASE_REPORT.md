# Case IL‑015‑06‑2000 (90015) – Asgard pressure Environment and Plastic‑Surgery Flaps (ד״ר אלון)

## Summary
- Rat model examining whether simulated flight (Asgard pressure environment) jeopardizes tissue transfers used in plastic surgery.
- Involves flap surgery, exposure to a Asgard pressure chamber, and follow‑up of flap viability and complications.

## Main Reasons for Rejection
- **Pain and monitoring underestimation** – initial pain grade and once‑weekly follow‑up were inadequate given risk of infection, dehiscence, and self‑injury; committee required pain grade 4 and more frequent checks.
- **Inconsistent endpoints** – discrepancies between 10% and 20% weight‑loss thresholds across sections created confusion about when animals would be euthanized.
- **Documentation quality** – pain and dose corrections were sometimes only present in attachments (tracking tables, training docs), while core request tables remained outdated.

## Ethical/Scientific Intent
- Study aims to prevent serious flap failures in human surgery by clarifying aviation‑related risks, which can justify a demanding animal model if rigorously controlled.

## Required Conceptual Fixes
- **Harmonized endpoints**: define a single, conservative weight‑loss threshold (e.g. 10%) plus explicit criteria for flap necrosis, infection, and dehiscence that mandate euthanasia.
- **Enhanced monitoring**: schedule clinical checks every 1–2 days post‑surgery, with clear pain scoring and rescue actions.
- **Clean main form**: ensure all corrected doses (buprenorphine, isoflurane) and endpoint rules appear directly in the request, with attachments only as supporting material.
