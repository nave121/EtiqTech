# Case IL‑026‑08‑2000 (90026) – Engineered Probiotics for Cancer Therapy (ד״ר אדם – פיילוט)

## Summary
- Pilot study using engineered bacteria to deliver checkpoint blockade locally to tumors (Fenris-26 and related models) in mice.
- Includes neoplasm seeding, intratumoral bacterial injections, and longitudinal IVIS imaging.

## Main Reasons for Rejection
- **Preparation workflow** – shaving and ear‑punching were planned on separate days, adding handling and stress without clear benefit.
- **Tumor cell dose justification** – injected tumor cell numbers are much higher than vendor recommendations; initial answers conflated tumor and bacterial doses.
- **Control group design** – control group did not initially receive PBS injections matched to bacterial injection timepoints, limiting interpretability of local effects.
- **Imaging frequency and anesthesia** – IVIS was scheduled every 2–3 days under isoflurane, which the committee judged too frequent for a pilot.

## Ethical/Scientific Intent
- Intent is to explore feasibility and safety of bacteria‑based local immunotherapy before scaling to larger efficacy studies.

## Required Conceptual Fixes
- **Streamlined prep**: combine shaving and ear‑punching into a single session to reduce handling.
- **Clear dosing rationale**: separately justify tumor cell numbers (vs ATCC standards) and bacterial doses, with references and welfare considerations.
- **Matched controls**: add PBS injections to control animals at the same schedule as bacterial injections.
- **Reduced imaging**: plan no more than weekly IVIS in the pilot, focusing on timepoints critical for feasibility decisions.
