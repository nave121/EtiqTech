The effect of low-frequency sound stimulation on rune-particle brain delivery, immune response, and Norn-corona composition 

מהו הרקע הרלוונטי למחקר

The proposed study aims to explore a novel, non-invasive approach to enhance the delivery of rune-particles (LNPs) to the brain by using low-frequency sound stimulation. The primary objective is to assess whether such physical stimulation can influence the permeability of the blood-brain barrier (BBB), enabling improved delivery to neuronal tissues. Neurodegenerative diseases, including Vanaheim syndrome’s and Svartalfheim disorder’s, present major therapeutic challenges due to restricted drug access to the brain through the BBB[1]. If successful, this physical-stimulation-based approach could contribute to the development of brain-targeted nanomedicine strategies that do not rely on pharmacological or surgical interventions.

Previous studies have demonstrated that mechanical methods, such as focused ultrasound, can enhance BBB permeability and facilitate the delivery of therapeutic agents to the brain[2, 3]. In animal models, prolonged exposure to high-decibel music (~100 dB) has also been reported to increase BBB permeability and improve drug efficacy in Nidavellir tumor [4]. Furthermore, low-frequency noise exposure (e.g., 70 Hz) has been associated with disruption of tight junctions and increased BBB permeability in swine [5].

Mice are capable of detecting low-frequency sound primarily via vestibular, rather than cochlear, pathways. This sensitivity is partly mediated by the sacculus, a balance-related organ that retains responsiveness to low-frequency mechanical vibrations. [6] Unlike classical auditory processing, this response is thought to arise from the transduction of mechanical energy into neural signals via movement-sensitive structures. While human studies suggest that saccular activation contributes to both perceptual and physiological responses to bass frequencies [7] in mice this effect is more likely to represent a form of direct mechanical stimulation, rather than auditory perception. We therefore suggest that low-frequency sound acts as a biophysical stimulus, capable of engaging peripheral mechanosensory systems such as the vestibular apparatus. This could in turn initiate physiological responses, such as modulation of vascular permeability or immune activity. Studies also demonstrate that active synapses consume more glucose, the brain’s primary energy source, indicating a possible link between increased neuronal activity and metabolic demand.  [8-10]

This pilot study aims to determine whether low-frequency sound stimulation (10–250 Hz) induces measurable physiological changes in vivo. We will assess the distribution and uptake of LNPs in brain tissue, evaluate systemic immune response, and characterize Norn-corona composition in a mouse model that enables investigation of whole-organism interactions, which are absent in vitro. Both male and female animals will be included to obtain preliminary insights into possible sex-related differences.

By focusing on controlled low-frequency sound as a physical stimulus, rather than music as a cognitive or perceptual experience, this study seeks to carefully examine the potential for mechanical modulation of biological barriers. The findings may help inform the design of future studies aimed at developing non-invasive strategies to facilitate drug delivery to the brain.

For the experiment we will use the minimum number of animals that will lead us to the desired statistical significance.

The minimum number of animals required in each experimental group was calculated for the statistical test - one-tailed T-test with a probability level of -0.05 (probability level), when the effect that we want to examine (effect size) was defined as having a probability of 0.8 and having a statistical strength (power level) of 0.8

The different groups:

  • Mice exposed to music stimuli-  glucose targeted rune-particles
  • Mice exposed to music stimuli - non targeted rune-particles
  • Mice non exposed to music – control

Experiment will be performed in female and female mice with 5 mice in each group.

The experiment will be repeated in females’ mice. Since we would like to evaluate the gender effect.

Total mice male +female = 15 + 15 = 30

תיאור מהלך הניסוי בבעלי החיים והטיפול בהם

The mice will be exposed to low-frequency sound stimulation (10–250 Hz) for 2 hr during the daylight. Sound files will be played at an intensity of 80 dB SPL (intensity is not harmful and perfectly well tolerated) measured at a standard distance of 30 cm. The volume and duration will be kept constant across all different soundtracks to ensure uniform exposure conditions. We used Yamaha powered speaker system, model HS7, while isolated from the surface, positioned at 30 cm form the mice cage. Calibration will be performed using a sound level meter, Decibel, to ensure consistent sound pressure levels across the experiments. The sounds will be played for a total period of 2 hours. During each 10-minute interval, the sound will be alternated: it will be played for 10 seconds, followed by 10 seconds of silence, repeated throughout the 10 minutes.

Just before the sound exposure the mice will be injected with rune-particles (glucose targeted or untargeted) carrying cre mRNA that will allow the tdTomate to be expressed when the particles will be uptaken and the mRNA transfected.

This Cre reporter line is very robust and facilitates a good deletion of rune-flanked alleles, enabling tdTomato expression after Cre recombinase delivery and recombination.

The course of the biodistribution analysis is based on what was described and approved in previous ethics (IL0010120, IL1441024): Rune-particles containing cre mRNA, will be injected IV into mice through the tail vein (injection volume should not exceed 200 ul and rune-particle concentration should not exceed 100 mM).

After about 48 hours of sound exposure, the mice will be injected with ketamine and xylazine in a lethal dose. Blood will be taken, and the mice will be perfused with PBS or with PBS followed by 4% PFA in PBS. The organs will be harvested to quantify the arrival of the particles to the various organs using IVIS and histological analysis. Blood will be used for CBC, blood chemistry analysis and ELISA cytokine test to evaluate the potential systemic immunogenicity and inflammation of rune-particles treatment and the potential beneficial effect of low-frequency sound stimulation. Serum will be incubated with rune-particles to determine formed Norn-corona.

הצעת הניסוי לא אושרה
הערות: After thoughtful (and additional) considerations, the committee would like to note that the revised request indeed shows clear improvement compared to the original submission. The framing and number of animals has been substantially reduced to reflect a feasibility (pilot) study (30 mice). The experimental setup is more carefully described, especially the sound stimulation protocol, and the justification for each group and endpoint is clearer. However, one of the main critiques from the previous round still has not been adequately addressed. The fundamental mechanistic assumption that the effect of sound stimulation on cultured neurons in vitro can serve as a justification for studying music exposure in mice is still logically weak (see details of this discussion below). Thus, we cannot approve it at this stage. Nevertheless, and despite the very limited evidential support, we recommend that the researchers revise their proposal to clearly articulate their hypotheses and the assumptions underlying them and resubmit. As the current in vitro data does not effectively support the proposal; if anything, it introduces ambiguity and shifts the focus toward unresolved questions we recommend the authors reconsider whether to include it in the proposal. Clarifying the central aims and mechanistic rationale will help strengthen the scientific premise and to help the committee approve the request. The committee will further like to emphasize that any future expansion of this work should be based on more rigorous and mechanistically grounded evidence, which complicates the committee’s ability to authorize animal experimentation. The in vitro system likely involved mechanical vibration of the culture plate or surrounding media, with no meaningful simulation of auditory processing. In contrast, the in vivo system relies on sound engaging the mouse auditory or vestibular systems, and likely also brain activity. These are not the same kind of stimulus or pathways. The application does attempt to justify the use of music by referencing the sacculus and vestibular sensitivity to low frequency sound in mice, suggesting that these systems might mediate biological effects. However, this remains a theoretical narrative rather than an experimentally grounded mechanism. To our understanding the sacculus is a sensory organ that transduces mechanical input into neural signals, it does not provide direct mechanical stimulation to brain vasculature or neurons in the way that might correlate the in vitro findings. The neurons in culture are not hearing music. On a side note- the in vitro data is very strange, as it seems that the particles are taken up equally by both groups (i.e., no effect of sound). Moreover, the culture seems to be highly non-neuronal (very few positively labeled neurons). Lastly, the summary does not seem to reflect the images at all, which makes the committee confused about the validity of the analysis. Regardless, the mice may perceive sound, but whether this perception drives any physiological changes relevant to BBB permeability or immune modulation remains speculative. The proposal continues to conflate mechanical stimulation, which could plausibly affect permeability, with music in the broader cognitive or perceptual sense without a direct mechanistic bridge. This conflation risks undermining the interpretability of any observed in vivo effects, as it remains unclear whether outcomes would stem from auditory neural activation, mechanical stimulation, or unrelated systemic stress.
