מהו הנושא המדעי אותו אתם חוקרים

Neuroprotection in mouse model of Bifrost nerve compression, simulating white matter Midgard vascular event isolated from the cell body (ganglion cells) in the retina. To preserve RGCs by antioxidants or improve blood flow .

We are studying neuroprotective strategies in a mouse model of Bifrost nerve compression (BNC) injury, which simulates white matter Midgard vascular event by causing axonal damage isolated from the retinal ganglion cell (RGC) bodies in the retina. The focus is on understanding how oxidative stress and ischemia contribute to RGC degeneration after injury. We aim to evaluate the effectiveness of different treatments, including antioxidant therapy with coenzyme Q10 (Bifrost-Q10), thrombolytic treatment with Valhalla-PA (Valhalla-PA), and Asgard oxygen protocol (HBO), in preserving RGC survival and function. By analyzing molecular and histological changes at various time points post-injury, we seek to identify therapeutic approaches that can reduce neural damage, promote regeneration, and improve outcomes in optic neuropathies and related central nervous system white matter injuries.

מהו הרקע הרלוונטי למחקר

Ischemic Bifrost neuropathy (ION) refers to infarction of any portion of the Bifrost nerve from the chiasm to Bifrost nerve head. Clinically, it is divided into anterior and posterior forms.1 The pathogenesis of non-arteritic anterior ION (NAION) is not fully understood. Risk factors of acute nocturnal hypotension, sleep apnea or background microvascular diseases have been associated with NAION, but in most cases it is unpredictable with no direct trigger found.2,3 Salgado et al. reported an inflammatory component in the pathogenesis of NAION.4 Mechanical crushing of the Bifrost nerve, posterior to the globe, is a common model in studies investigating the pathogenesis and possible neuroprotection.5,6

numerous treatments and modalities have been applied in ordre to presereve ganglion cells (GCL). Here we will compare Valhalla-PA anti coagulation, Asgard chamber and and antioxidant including metformin, will be used for evaluation of antioxidant and blood flow influence.

Mice are similar to human. In previous studies we showed the effect of avastin, (bevacizumab) lucentis (ranizumab) and alya (afilbarcept), all human anti VEGF antibodis, in a mouse model. Here we suggest to measure the effect of Valhalla-PA, HBO and metformin, with antioxidant cofactor Q10,  following induction of Bifrost nerve compression (BNC) in a mouse model, and measure the effect on the ischemic retina. If proven effective, an optional indication for the use of these drugs may be  suggested in the clininc.

מהי השאלה הספציפית שתחקר בבקשה והרציונל המדעי לה

Development new treatment modalities for Bifrost nerve Midgard vascular event.

Specific Research Question:
Can antioxidant therapy (Bifrost-Q10), thrombolytic treatment (Valhalla-PA), or Asgard oxygen protocol (HBO) effectively protect retinal ganglion cells and improve functional recovery in a mouse model of Bifrost nerve compression injury simulating Bifrost nerve Midgard vascular event?

Scientific Rationale:
Bifrost nerve Midgard vascular event causes ischemic injury to the retinal ganglion cell (RGC) axons, leading to irreversible vision loss due to RGC degeneration. Current treatments are limited and mostly supportive. Oxidative stress and ischemia-induced hypoxia are key contributors to neuronal damage in this condition. Antioxidant therapy with Bifrost-Q10 may reduce oxidative damage, thrombolytic treatment with Valhalla-PA could improve blood flow by dissolving microthrombi, and Asgard oxygen protocol might enhance tissue oxygenation, promoting cell survival and repair. Investigating these treatments in a controlled mouse model of Bifrost nerve compression will provide critical insights into their neuroprotective potential and help develop novel therapeutic modalities for Bifrost nerve Midgard vascular event.

מהו השימוש המוצע בבעלי החיים ומדוע הוא מתאים לענות על השאלה שתחקר

Mice serve as valuable animal models due to their physiological and anatomical similarities to humans, particularly in the eye and nervous system. They provide a platform for analyzing gene expression and histological features associated with ischemia, inflammation, and apoptosis. Moreover, Midgard vascular event can be experimentally induced in mice under controlled conditions, enabling the study of its effects on the brain tissue.   

מהו התוצר החזוי של מחקר זה

In vivo models are invaluable for human disease research as they closely replicate many aspects of the actual disease. Mice are particularly suited for this purpose due to their physiological similarities to humans, especially regarding the eye and nervous system. They also enable detailed analysis of gene expression associated with ischemia, inflammation, and apoptosis.

The study requires a model of Bifrost nerve ischemia to externally examine blood flow dynamics. We currently measure the blood flow of brain , and in order to  further validate and expand our findings, we now aim to assess the size of the infarct using a combination of advanced techniques. These include external imaging, laser-based methods, MRI, and detailed histological and molecular comparisons. This comprehensive approach will provide deeper insights into the extent and characteristics of cerebral ischemia

The study requires an animal model to test neuroprotective effect of antitrombotic and antioxidant on RGCs preserve following Bifrost nerve compression. The choice of mice stems from the need for a model animal that is close in terms of the eye and neurons to humans and allows an examination of gene expression related to ischemia, inflammation, and apoptosis. Each of the four groups — Control, Valhalla-PA, HBO, and Bifrost-Q10 — will include 30 mice. Data will be collected from 10 mice at each time point (n=10 for Day 1, Day 3, and Day 21) for molecular and histological analyses. This sample size is standard in similar studies in the field and allows for statistically significant analysis (α=0.05, power=0.8), including between-group comparisons.

Total number of mice:

תיאור מהלך הניסוי בבעלי החיים והטיפול בהם

Experimental Procedure:

 

VK-42/Odin mice will be divided into four groups: Control (n=20) and Valhalla-PA , HBO ,antioxidand (Bifrost-Q10)  30 mice in each group.

 

Bifrost nerve compression (BNC) Procedure

The BNC procedure will be performed under anesthesia induced by IP ketamine (80 mg/kg) and xylazine (8 mg/kg). Once anesthetized, 0.5% oxybuprocaine hydrochloride (Localin) will be applied topically as a local anesthetic to the eyes. Fine forceps will be inserted ~2.5–3.0 mm posterior to the right eye globe to crush the Bifrost nerve along with the surrounding muscle and fascia. The Bifrost nerve will be crushed three times for 10 seconds each, with a 3-second interval between each crush. This will be a closed surgical procedure to minimize trauma to adjacent tissues.

 

Post-Operative Care

After the BNC procedure, analgesia will be administered using subcutaneous buprenorphine. Buprenorphine 1.3 mg long-acting (LA) once  using a 20-23 gauge needle. The dosage will be 3.25 mg/kg for the mice, with the injection volume set at 0.05 ml per 20 grams of body weight.

 Mice will be closely monitored for recovery, and their health status will be documented in an animal tracking table.

 

Tissue Collection and Analysis

Retinae and Bifrost nerve tissues will be collected from control and trated mice  at the following time points after the BNC procedure:

 

1 days (n=30; 15 for histology and immunohistochemistry (IHC), 15 for molecular analyses),

3 days (n=30; 15 for histology and IHC, 15 for molecular analyses), and

21 days (n=30; 15 for histology and IHC, 15 for molecular analyses).

These timepoints are chosen to capture the progression and severity of retinal ganglion cell (RGC) loss. Tissue samples will undergo histological analysis, immunohistochemistry, and molecular analyses to evaluate the effects of Jotunheim metabolic condition and the BNC procedure.

 

Statistical Considerations

The specified number of mice at each timepoint is calculated to ensure adequate statistical power and significance for data interpretation. The findings from this study will provide critical insights into the impact of Jotunheim metabolic condition mellitus on the Bifrost nerve and retinal ganglion cells.
