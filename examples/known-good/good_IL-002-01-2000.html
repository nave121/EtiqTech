Early detection of pancreatic dysplasia and cancer

מהו הרקע הרלוונטי למחקר

, Pancreatic cancer is the third leading cause of cancer-related death in the US, surpassing breast cancer, and has the highest mortality rate of all major cancers. In 2024, in the USA, about 66,440 people (34,530 men and 31,910 women) will be diagnosed with pancreatic cancer, and about 51,750 people (27,270 men and 24,480 women) will die of pancreatic cancer.

Pancreatic cancer is curable if detected early but is typically asymptomatic until the tumor has metastasized or is too large to be successfully treated. Those few patients who do survive pancreatic cancer typically had a very small tumor (<1cm) that was accidentally discovered on a CT or MRI that they received for an unrelated reason. An easily performed, accurate screening test for very small pancreatic tumors would

increase survival rates dramatically and would be readily adopted by gastroenterologists and their patients.

Adenocyte is introducing two proprietary improvements to a pancreatic cancer detection method that was investigated from 1974-1995 but was not pursued further due to a sensitivity of only 70%. Based on extensive clinical experience with over 500,000 patients in other body sites, Adenocyte believes that test sensitivity with these two improvements will exceed 95%, resulting in reliable early detection of the vast majority of pancreatic cancers when they are still very small and highly curable.

The epithelial surface of the exocrine glands that typically cause pancreatic cancer is naturally exfoliated (sloughed off) and regenerated approximately every three weeks. All of these discarded cells are drained into a single duct that exits the pancreas at one small point, the ampulla of Vater. Thus, the pancreatic fluid that exits the ampulla for the duodenum (small intestine) contains representative cells that are constantly and naturally shed into it from the entire exocrine pancreas.

Within two minutes after intravenous administration of the hormone Secretin, over 25 mL of pancreatic fluid can be easily collected from the ampulla by a gastroenterologist using a standard duodenoscope, and this fluid can then be microscopically examined at the laboratory for the presence of exfoliated dysplastic or cancer cells. In prior studies, the sensitivity of this method to detect pancreatic cancer was limited to 70% due to the relatively low number of cancer cells in the fluid, and the fact that they that the pathologist was required to manually search the slide for these few “needles in a haystack”.

The scientists, pathologists, and engineers associated with Adenocyte pioneered the application of advanced high-speed computer imaging systems to detect rare abnormal cells in a variety of complex tissue specimens. Adenocyte also has exclusive rights to a patent-pending method by which the external application of focused ultrasound (US) energy prior to secretin administration is used to greatly increase the number of exocrine cells exfoliated into the pancreatic fluid.

Animals will be selected for the study after health screening and complete blood tests. Each animal will be sampled 48 hours before and 48 hours after the procedure for blood count and biochemistry panel to evaluate the general health status of the animal before and after the treatment. Blood collection will be performed after sedation with ketamin and xylazine according to the protocol below, 3ml of blood . Using blood samples, we will evaluate the hematology for a possible inflammation and the biochemistry for pancreatic enzymes such as lipase and amylase. In case of deviations before the study the animal will be excluded from the study. In case of deviation after the treatment the result will be compared to the results prior to the study and conclusions will be drawn.

In the three days prior to the procedure animals will be prepared for endoscopy using  liquid diet (Ensure).

 

Anesthesia protocol:

Premedication : Xylazine  1 mg/kg + Ketamine 20 mg/kg I.M

Induction: Propofol 4 – 6 mg/kg I.V.

Maintenance : Isoflurane 1 – 3% delivered by P.P.V.
