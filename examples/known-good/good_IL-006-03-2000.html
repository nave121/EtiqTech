מהו הנושא המדעי אותו אתם חוקרים

Analyzing the therapeutic potential of a cell-based treatment, which is derived from activated mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) in pulmonary fibrosis (PF) murine model: Efficacy, mechanism of action, biodistribution and tolerability.

מהו הרקע הרלוונטי למחקר

Idiopathic Pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a complex, progressing, and irreversible interstitial lung disease where the lungs become scarred and as a result, the respiratory function is impaired in a life threatened manner. The symptoms of the disease include severe shortness of breath, hoarseness, weakness and loss without quantity. In fact, the respiratory function is seriously damaged in a life-threatening manner. Lung tissue scarring occurs due to extracellular matrix (ECM) modification including changes in fibrous lung composition and aggregation of collagen, elastin and other fibers. IPF can be induced by various clinical conditions such as lung injury, inflammatory processes, pulmonary diseases, or exposure to radiation and chemicals. IPF is considered an incurable disease with median survival of 3.8 years after diagnosis. To date there are no effective treatments for the disease, and most patients do not improve despite being treated and gradually develop respiratory failure. Current existing treatments include oxygen, steroids, and drugs such as nintedanib (Ofev) and pirfenidone (Esbriet). Yet these therapies are ineffective, and lung transplantation is the only effective solution in the end-stage phase of the disease. Therefore, there is a real need to continue to study the pathological pathways underlying this devastating disease, and to find new treatments for the disease in order to minimize the progression of IPF while causing lung regeneration and restoration of pulmonary function. Cell therapy with mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) is an innovative treatment for the treatment of various fibrotic diseases that is being tested in clinical trials. However, treatment with MSCs is complicated in the case of IPF. Furthermore, there is an inherent risk in treatment with mesenchymal stem cells since they can be changed. We have developed a method in which we grow the MSCs in culture, activate them, and collect the conditioned-medium (CM). This CM contains secreted molecules that include various anti-fibrotic factors. In this study, we will examine the efficacy of activated-MSCs-CM treatment on PF induced in mice. We will study the mechanism underlying the therapeutic effect. In addition, we will evaluate the biodistribution of the treatment and if the treatment is toxic. This study will demonstrate the potential use of activated-MSCs-CM as a plausible treatment for IPF.

מהי השאלה הספציפית שתחקר בבקשה והרציונל המדעי לה

The specific aims: (A) Evaluation of activated-MSCs-CM treatment efficacy on pulmonary fibrosis (PF). (B) Study the mechanisms of action (MOA) of the treatment. (C) To study the biodistribution of the treatment. (D) To study the tolerability to the treatment.

מהו השימוש המוצע בבעלי החיים ומדוע הוא מתאים לענות על השאלה שתחקר

Mice will be employed in this study.

מהו התוצר החזוי של מחקר זה

This study will highlight the potential use of activated-MSCs-CM as a treatment for IPF, a lethal disease. This will be achieved by finding the pathological pathways that occur in the fibrotic lung tissue and by studying the mechanism by which activated-MSC-CM inhibit these pathologies in order to delay PF disease progression, and to increase tissue repair and regeneration. Furthermore, this study will provide additional insight on the tolerability to the activated-MSC-derived CM in PF murine model.

The study will involve mice. Mice are the ultimate use for studying treatments for PF, as they are low vertebrate that can mimic human pathological conditions. In this study, immunodeficient NK-8/Hel mice are chosen to study the therapeutic effect of activated-MSCs-CM, on bleomycin-induced fibrosis, since we will be using non-self-MSCs-CM as a treatment for IPF. In the experiments, we will use live imaging technique (i.e., micro-CT) to monitor disease progression. In the Tox studies (D), VK-42/Odin mice will be used, as they possess a complete immune system, which is important for the study of toxicity.

As can be appreciate from the number of experiments that need to be performed in the aims, the number of mice per experiment is quite limited, but enough to obtain statistical power, as per the regulation written by the NIH. The sample size for each experiment is designed to have 80% power at a two-sided α of 0.05.  In bleomycin-induced pulmonary fibrosis model, we need at least 15 mice per group in order to reach the above mentioned statistical. This is extremely important, as disease establishment and progression following bleomycin induction can be divers due to technical issues. In non-diseased, naive mice (toxicology studies) n=7 mice per group is sufficient.

In sections A and B of the following experiments, we will induce pulmonary fibrosis (PF) by a single bleomycin administration in NK-8/Hel mice as elaborated below under Experimental Procedures. Treatment with human activated-MSCs-CM is also described under that chapter. It is important to note that while the MSCs are activated in-vitro with anti-cancer agent (e.g., paclitaxel), the conditioned media (CM) collected and that is further used in the in-vivo mice experiments, does not contain any trace of the activation agent (i.e., no anti-cancer drug is given directly to the mice).

The following experiments will be conducted in this ethic: 

(A) Efficacy study.

(i) To evaluate the use of activated-MSCs derived CM as a treatment for PF. Human MSCs * collected from healthy donors by RoosterBio (US) will be employed for the CM production that will be further be used in mice*. We will study escalating doses of the CM in bleomycin-induced PF in NK-8/Hel mice. Treatment groups will be therefore: (i) activated-MSCs-CM at 2 treatments; 4 treatments; 6 treatments. (ii) non-activated-MSCs-CM – at 2 treatments; 4 treatments; 6 treatments (iii) mice treated with saline (treatment control), (iv) non-PF/non-treated mice (healthy controls). All together 8 groups.

Experiential and general procedures:

The following procedures will be performed on the mice:

1) Induction of PF: PF model will be induced in NK-8/Hel mice by a single intratracheally (IT) instillation bleomycin (Sigma) dissolved in saline (1mg/kg in 50μl saline per mouse). The bleomycin will be instilled into the tracheal lumen through a gavage needle (24G) under isoflurane (2.5% in oxygen) anesthesia. PF is established after 14 days.

Elaborated Procedure:

Isoflurane-anesthetized mouse will be attached by the front teeth on a wire attached to a 45° angled fiberglass stand (supine head up). Then the tongue will be gently lift out of the mouth using sterile, blunt-ended forceps and will be held this way during the procedure to prevent accidental crushing of the tongue. While holding the mouse’s tongue, the gavage needle is inserted gently through the glottal opening into the trachea (while the gavage needle is connected to a syringe - w/o a plunger- filled with 100μl H2O; water moving up and down as mouse breathes is a sign that the needle is inserted into trachea).  Then, the syringe attached to the gavage needle is disconnect and using a micropipette, the bleomycin (50μl saline; 1mg/kg) is applied at the basis of the gavage needle. The bleomycin is then pushed into trachea using a 500μL syringe filled with air via the gavage needle, in order to allow direct targeting of the lungs. Following instillation, the tongue is released, and the needle is taken out. The mouse is then removed from the stand, placed back in the cage, and monitored until recovery from anaesthesia completely.

2) Treatments intratracheally (IT) Administration: At day 15 post bleomycin induction of PF, treatment with CM derived from activated or non-activated MSCs or saline will be initiated. For this 50 μl/mouse of either MSCs-CM or saline will be instilled into the tracheal lumen through a gavage needle (24G) under isoflurane (2.5% in oxygen) anesthesia, in a similar way as described above.

3) Micro-CT live imaging procedure: Mice will be anesthetized with isoflurane by an induction in a concentration of 4% per litter, followed by maintenance that will be performed in a dose of 2-3% isoflurane per litter. Micro-CT imaging will take place at day 0 (before bleomycin induction; day 14 (when PF is apparent); and 3 weeks following treatment initiation (5 weeks post bleomycin induction) just before seriation, per mouse along the experiment. In addition to images that will be taken, breathing rates will be recorded as well.

4) Respiratory functions and oxygen saturation: Breathing rate (breath/min) and oxygen saturation (%SpO2) in mice will be assessed prior to and following bleomycin induction, and just before the termination phase of each treatment cycle. (i) Respiratory rate will be assessed using video recordings and compared to untreated bleomycin induction mice. To minimize movement during recording, each mouse will be placed in a restrainer. A 30 sec slow-motion video will be then recorded to count their breaths. Oxygen saturation will be measured by using the Small Animal Physiological Monitoring System (Harvard Apparatus, Inc; Cat. No. 75-1500), found in our unit. Each animal will be placed on the monitor platform with its paws on the metal pads. The sensor will be placed on the tail of the animal. SpO2 signals will be recorded for 20 sec, and will be evaluated for to untreated BLM-exposed mice.

5) Facial vein bleeding: Each mouse is anesthetized using open-drop exposure to isoflurane inhalant anesthesia for induction using a concentration of 2-3%. The mouse is held by the back of the neck, and the loose skin of the head is tightened with the thumb and middle finger and the mouse is placed on the wire bars of the cage. A 4mm blood lancet is used for the bleeding procedure. The tip of the lancet is pointed right over the mouse chick at the hairless freckle on the side of the jaw, and a short and sharp thrust is then applied. As soon as the chick is punctured, blood is dropped into a pre-containing heparin small blood collecting tube. When the allowable amount of blood is collected, a piece of gauze on the chick is used to prevent further bleeding, and the mouse is returned to its cage.

6) Bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) technique: Mice will be scarified by isoflurane overdosing*. Then mice were injected with 1 ml PBS via the trachea, and immediately the bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) is collected. This procedure is repeated once more. Thereafter, the BALF will be separated for soluble fraction and cellular fraction by centrifugation at 600 x g. Of note, no cervical dislocation is employed for this experiment since this procedure might damage the integrity and purity of the BALF by blood cells.
