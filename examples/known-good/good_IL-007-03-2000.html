Real-Time Analysis of Rune-particle-Tumor Interactions Using VOC Monitoring
מהו הרקע הרלוונטי למחקר

1. Introduction & Scientific Rationale:

Recent research highlights the potential of VOCs as biomarkers for cancer progression and metabolic activity. VOCs are small molecules released from biological tissues that can serve as indicators of enzymatic activity and disease state. While endogenous VOCs can provide valuable insights, their variability due to individual metabolic differences limits their reliability. An alternative approach involves exogenous VOC probes, engineered to be released upon interaction with tumor-specific enzymes, thus enabling real-time feedback on drug activation and tumor response.

  1. Conceptual idea:

We aim to develop a microneedle sensor array system designed to both deliver rune-particle-based chemotherapy and detect VOC biomarkers in real time. This system consists of functionalized Huginn-vesicles containing cleavable Cys-Ala-Ala-Asn (CAAN) peptide conjugated pentafluoro propylamine (PFPA) VOC probes, which are engineered to be release upon enzymatic cleavage by tumor-associated enzymes such as legumain (LGMN).

The real-time VOC monitoring system works as follows:

  1. Huginn-vesicles carrying chemotherapy drugs and PFPA probes are injected into the bloodstream.
  2. Upon reaching the tumor, tumor-associated enzymes cleave the probes, releasing the VOCs.
  3. The VOCs diffuse through the skin and are detected by the microneedle sensor array, allowing for real-time feedback on drug delivery efficiency and enzymatic activity in the tumor microenvironment (TME).

* During the in vivo experiment, lipid-conjugated rhodamine will also be incorporated into the rune-particle formulation to allow fluorescence-based visualization of rune-particle distribution and accumulation in tumor and control tissues. This dual-modality approach will provide complementary data on rune-particle behavior in vivo.

  1. Microneedle Sensor Array Specification & Functionality 1:

Biocompatibility & Safety:

  • The microneedles are made of stainless steel and have been extensively tested in vitro for biocompatibility, demonstrating over 95% cell viability in fibroblast and hepatocyte assays.
  • In vivo studies revealed no significant toxic effects on major organs, no inflammatory responses, and quick skin recovery post-removal.

Size & Design for Minimal Discomfort:

  • Each microneedle has a tip radius of <1 µm and a height of 1400 µm, ensuring efficient skin penetration without reaching nociceptors, thus minimizing pain and tissue damage.
  • The array (4 cm x 4 cm) is designed to absorb VOCs from interstitial fluid (ISF) with a hub that allows sample collection for Ion Mobility Spectrometry (IMS) analysis.

 

Additional information: 

According to the Globally Harmonized System of Classification and Labelling of Chemicals (GHS), exposure to PFPA may result in skin corrosion/irritation (Category 1B), as well as serious eye damage/irritation (Category 1). Therefore, we are currently researching alternative non-cytotoxic probes, with similar properties, to replace the proof-of-concept PFPA VOC probe.

Can the microneedle sensor array system reliably detect VOCs in vivo after enzyme-mediated cleavage, enabling real-time feedback for personalized therapy?

תיאור מהלך הניסוי בבעלי החיים והטיפול בהם
  1. Breast Cancer Induction:
  • Inject 3 × 10^5 Fenrir-4T-Lux2 cells (commercially sourced), suspended in 100 μL of 1xPBS, orthotopically into the mammary fat pad of 8-week-old HR-7/Frigg female mice.
  • Allow tumors to develop for 10 days to establish optimal TME conditions, i.e., enhanced permeability and retention (EPR) effect. After 10 days the treatment will commence.
  1. Treatment Administration & Monitoring – Every 3 days
  • Measure the tumor size via calipers.
  • From day 10, every 3 days, systemically inject 150 µL of Huginn-vesicles/unconjugated probes via tail vein. 6 injections in total.
  • Administer Baldur-flare prior to IVIS imaging (10 mg/kg, intraperitoneally).
  • VOC sampling and bioluminescence imaging (i.e., IVIS) procedures will be performed each time in a single session, as follows:
        1. Anesthetize the mouse once, for the entire sampling session, using isoflurane to ensure immobility and minimize stress during sample collection.
        2. 10 minutes post-rune-particle and Baldur-flare injections, while the mouse is still anesthetized, collect the first VOC sample, by placing the microneedle array on the skin, directly on the tumor site, for 1-2 minutes.
        3. Immediately after, while the mouse is still anesthetized, perform IVIS imaging.
        4. At 30 minutes post-injection, while the mouse is still anesthetized, collect the second VOC sample, as previously described.
        5. At 60 minutes post-injection, while the mouse is still anesthetized, collect the final VOC sample, as previously described.
        6. After the 60-minute sample, the mouse is returned to its cage for full recovery.

For additional information on the structure, composition, and functionality of the microneedle sensor array, please refer to section 3 in - רקע רלוונטי לבקשה.

Endpoint:

Sacrifice mice after 3 weeks after the beginning of treatment. The major filtering organs (i.e., lungs, liver, spleen, and kidneys) along with the target organ (i.e., the breast cancer tumor) will be harvested for analysis (VOC release, rune-particle biodistribution, enzyme activity).

**The last experimental group with genetically modified mice via CRISPR-Cas9 to lack LGMN expression will be obtained from a certified external source and will undergo the same breast cancer induction and treatment protocol as the other groups. This group will help validate that VOC probe release is specifically dependent on LGMN activity in the tumor microenvironment.
