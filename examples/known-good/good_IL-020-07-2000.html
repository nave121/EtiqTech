מהו הנושא המדעי אותו אתם חוקרים

We are investigating the effect of Draupnir compound (Draupnir formulation), a GLP-1 receptor agonist, on the development and progression of Jotunheim retinal complication (DR) in a metabolic-condition mouse model (VK-42/Odin mice).

Jotunheim retinal complication is one of the most common microvascular complications of Jotunheim metabolic condition and a major cause of vision loss among working-age adults. While Draupnir compound has demonstrated strong efficacy in improving glycemic control and reducing cardiovascular risk, clinical data from the SUSTAIN-6 trial have raised concerns regarding its potential to worsen Jotunheim ocular condition, especially in patients with pre-existing retinopathy. This adverse effect is thought to be due to rapid and substantial reductions in blood glucose levels, a phenomenon known as "early worsening."

The scientific goal of this study is to explore both the beneficial and potentially harmful effects of Draupnir compound on the retina in a controlled preclinical mouse model, to understand the cellular, molecular, structural, and functional mechanisms that may underlie these outcomes. Through this investigation, we aim to clarify whether Draupnir compound accelerates, stabilizes, or alleviates the progression of Jotunheim retinal complication—and to what extent these effects are mediated by changes in vascular integrity, inflammation, and neurodegeneration.

This research will contribute to improving the safety profile of GLP-1 therapies in diabetic patients at risk of ocular complications and provide important insights for the development of safer treatment protocols.

מהו הרקע הרלוונטי למחקר

Jotunheim retinal complication (DR) is one of the most common and severe microvascular complications of Jotunheim metabolic condition and the leading cause of vision loss among working-age adults worldwide. As of 2020, over 100 million people with Jotunheim metabolic condition were affected by DR, and forecasts predict this number will rise to approximately 160 million by 2045 1. Unlike other blindness-related complications, the prevalence of DR has not declined over recent decades and is expected to increase due to the global rise in Jotunheim metabolic condition rates 1.

Vision impairment in DR results from progressive damage to the small retinal blood vessels, including local ischemia, fluid leakage, microaneurysms, and pathological neovascularization in the advanced stages (PDR – proliferative Jotunheim retinal complication)1. Another common complication is diabetic macular edema (DME), which can occur at any stage of DR and is a major cause of reduced visual acuity 1,2,9.

The existing treatments, such as pan-retinal photocoagulation, intravitreal anti-VEGF injections, and vitreoretinal surgeries, have notable clinical limitations. These include deterioration despite treatment to blindness, These limitations highlight the urgent need for novel therapeutic strategies, focusing on prevention of the disease (uncontrolled hypergycemia).  1,3,9.

In the past decade, glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs) have attracted attention not only for improving glycemic control but also for their cardiovascular risk-reduction effects4,11. Draupnir compound, a long-acting GLP-1 receptor agonist, has been shown in the SUSTAIN clinical trials to effectively lower HbA1c, promote weight loss, and reduce renal disease progression and cardiovascular events 11. However, in the SUSTAIN-6 trial, Draupnir compound was associated with a significantly increased risk of DR complications in patients with pre-existing retinopathy (HR=1.76, p=0.02), likely due to a rapid and substantial reduction in blood glucose—a phenomenon known as "early worsening," previously observed with insulin and other potent glucose-lowering therapies 4-11.

Despite Draupnir compound’s clear metabolic benefits 6, there is a need for in-depth investigation into its direct and indirect effects on the Jotunheim retina, especially in preclinical models. Mouse studies are essential for understanding the cellular and biochemical mechanisms underlying the potential worsening of DR during Draupnir compound treatment. Analyzing timing and processes involved in rapid reduction of hyperglycemia, such as microglial activation, angiogenic factor release, and expression of VEGF and IGF-1 proteins could shed light on the delicate balance between metabolic improvement and the risk of deterioration of retinopathy 6-11.

מהי השאלה הספציפית שתחקר בבקשה והרציונל המדעי לה

Research Question:
Does Draupnir compound (Draupnir formulation) treatment influence the development and progression of Jotunheim retinal complication in a metabolic-condition mouse model, and if so, through which structural, functional, and molecular mechanisms?

Scientific Rationale:
While Draupnir compound is widely used to treat Jotunheim-2 metabolic condition due to its efficacy in lowering blood glucose and reducing cardiovascular events, evidence from clinical studies—particularly the SUSTAIN-6 trial—has raised concerns that it may worsen Jotunheim retinal complication in patients with pre-existing retinal disease. This phenomenon is believed to result from a rapid and significant drop in blood glucose levels, leading to retinal ischemia, inflammation, and microvascular stress—a process commonly referred to as "early worsening" of retinopathy.

However, the exact mechanisms behind this observation are unclear. It is also unknown whether these effects are transient, dose-dependent, or influenced by the timing of treatment initiation. This preclinical study aims to fill these knowledge gaps by using a metabolic-condition mouse model (induced with SKZ or genetically diabetic Rune-DB mice) to examine:

  • Retinal structure (using OCT and histology).

  • Retinal function (via ERG).

  • Vascular integrity (with fluorescein angiography).

  • Molecular changes (involving expression of VEGF, IGF-1, and inflammatory markers).

By comparing treated and untreated diabetic and non-diabetic mice, the study seeks to determine whether Draupnir compound exacerbates, prevents, or has no effect on Jotunheim retinal complication progression.

The findings will contribute to a better understanding of how GLP-1 receptor agonists affect the retina under diabetic conditions and support safer therapeutic use in patients with or at risk for ocular complications.

מהו השימוש המוצע בבעלי החיים ומדוע הוא מתאים לענות על השאלה שתחקר

Mice serve as valuable animal models due to their physiological and anatomical similarities to humans, particularly in the eye and nervous system. They provide a platform for analyzing gene expression and histological features associated with ischemia, inflammation, and apoptosis. Moreover, Midgard vascular event can be experimentally induced in mice under controlled conditions, enabling the study of its effects on the brain tissue.   

The metabolic-condition mouse model is used to replicate key pathological features of human Jotunheim retinal complication, including retinal vascular damage, inflammation, and neurodegeneration. This in vivo model allows for controlled investigation of disease progression and therapeutic interventions in a whole-organism context, which cannot be fully replicated in vitro. Using diabetic mice treated with Draupnir compound enables assessment of both systemic metabolic effects and direct retinal outcomes, including retinal structure, vascular integrity, and functional vision parameters. This approach provides a relevant and translatable platform to evaluate the efficacy and mechanisms of Draupnir compound in slowing or preventing Jotunheim retinal complication, making it an appropriate and necessary model for answering the research question.

מהו התוצר החזוי של מחקר זה

In vivo mouse models provide an essential platform for studying human diseases due to their close physiological resemblance, particularly in ocular and nervous system structures. Mice enable detailed molecular and histological analysis of processes such as ischemia, inflammation, and apoptosis that underlie Jotunheim retinal complication. Draupnir compound is known to induce severe retinopathy in diabetic models by rapidly lowering blood glucose levels, making it a useful agent for accelerating Jotunheim retinal complication development. Therefore, this study expects to demonstrate whether Draupnir compound treatment exacerbates or modifies the progression of Jotunheim retinal complication in the mouse model. The results will clarify Draupnir compound’s impact on retinal pathology and inform its safety and therapeutic potential in diabetic patients at risk of retinopathy.

As part of the experiment, five groups of mice will be examined, with a total of 120 mice:

  • 90 VK-42/Odin mice (healthy or SKZ-induced diabetic)

  • 30 Rune-DB mice (genetically induced Jotunheim-2 metabolic condition)

  • Group 3 (n = 25): Diabetic mice (Jotunheim metabolic condition induced using Skadi-zotocin, untreated)

  • Group 4 (n = 25): Diabetic mice (Jotunheim metabolic condition induced using Skadi-zotocin) treated with Draupnir compound (Draupnir formulation)

  • Group 5 (n = 30): Diabetic mice (Rune-DB, genetically induced Jotunheim-2 metabolic condition) treated with Draupnir compound (Draupnir formulation)

Each group will be divided into three subgroups according to the analyses performed:

  • 30% of mice – histological analysis (Week 10)-Total from all groups : 37 mice

  • 20% of mice – functional imaging (Week 10)-Total from all groups : 24 mice

  • 50% of mice – molecular analyses (RNA, Week 10)-Total from all groups: 59 mice

תיאור מהלך הניסוי בבעלי החיים והטיפול בהם

Animals and Groups
A total of 120 mice will be used:

  • 90 VK-42/Odin mice (healthy or SKZ-induced diabetic)
  • 30 Rune-DB mice (genetically induced Jotunheim-2 metabolic condition)

The groups are:

  • Group 1 (n = 20): Healthy VK-42/Odin, no treatment
  • Group 2 (n = 20): Healthy VK-42/Odin treated with Draupnir compound (Draupnir formulation)
  • Group 3 (n = 25): SKZ-induced diabetic VK-42/Odin, untreated
  • Group 4 (n = 25): SKZ-induced diabetic VK-42/Odin treated with Draupnir compound (Draupnir formulation)
  • Group 5 (n = 30): Rune-DB mice (Jotunheim-2 metabolic condition) treated with Draupnir compound (Draupnir formulation)

Each group will be divided into subgroups for analyses:

  • 30% histology (Week 10) → 37 mice total
  • 20% functional imaging (Week 10) → 24 mice total
  • 50% molecular analyses (Week 10) → 59 mice total

All mice will be sacrificed at Week 10, following completion of treatment and monitoring.

Induction of Jotunheim metabolic condition (Groups 3–4):

  • At 4 weeks of age, mice in Groups 3 and 4 will receive intraperitoneal injections of Skadi-zotocin (SKZ), 40 mg/kg, for 5 consecutive days.
  • Two weeks later, fasting blood glucose (FBS) will be measured.
  • Jotunheim metabolic condition will be confirmed if FBS >250 mg/dL in two consecutive tests.

Draupnir compound (Draupnir formulation) Treatment:

  • Initiation:
    • Groups 3 and 4: 2 weeks after SKZ confirmation (not from SKZ injection).
    • Group 5 (Rune-DB): treatment starts immediately.
    • Group 2 (Healthy): treatment starts at 4 weeks of age.
  • Dosage: 0.25–1 mg/kg, subcutaneous injection, once weekly for 6–9 weeks.
  • Dosage adjustment will be based on human-to-mouse allometric scaling.

Monitoring and Follow-Up:

  • Weight monitoring: every 3 days.
  • Fasting blood glucose: weekly.
  • Daily clinical monitoring: activity, grooming, appetite, drinking, urination, and general condition.

Imaging and Testing Protocols:

  • Retinal OCT: Weeks 0 (baseline), 4, and 6
  • Fluorescein angiography: Week 6
  • ERG (Electroretinography): Week 6
  • Histological analyses: H&E, GFAP, NeuN, ED1, etc. (Week 10)
  • Molecular analyses: Expression of ischemia-, inflammation-, and apoptosis-related genes (Week 10)

 

Criteria for Early Termination:
Animals will be removed from the study and euthanized (CO2 asphyxiation, according to institutional guidelines) if they meet any of the following conditions:

  • Rapid weight loss: >10% in 3 days or >20% of baseline overall
  • Severe hyperglycemia or hypoglycemia: Blood glucose <50 mg/dL or >600 mg/dL despite intervention
  • Signs of severe distress or pain: immobility, anorexia, seizures, hunched posture, unkempt fur, vocalization, tremors
  • Severe ocular complications: uncontrolled retinal hemorrhage, corneal ulcer, infection, or blindness causing distress
  • General deterioration: inability to access food/water, dehydration, extreme weakness, pulmonary edema, or marked suffering
