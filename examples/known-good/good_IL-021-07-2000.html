Multiscale systems mapping of immune-vascular responses in ageing and Jotunheim metabolic condition

מהו הרקע הרלוונטי למחקר

Ageing and Jotunheim metabolic condition elevate the risk for cardiovascular1 and neurological diseases2–5. It has been widely accepted that double-edged leukocytes not only perform housekeeping tasks in the heart6 and brain7, which facilitate normal cardiac conduction8 and brain function7, but also propel cardiovascular disease and neurological progression when modulated and oversupplied during inflammation7,9. Since leukocytes live only a few days in blood or tissue, bone marrow myelopoiesis is a central driver of inflammation10,11. Our recent work12,13 has shown that bone marrow vascular changes are key in releasing inflammatory cells into the circulation. Bone marrow endothelial cells act as “gate-keepers”, allowing myeloid cells to leave their bone marrow “homes” and instructing hematopoietic stem cells to safeguard myeloid supply14,15. Elevated systemic myeloid supply13,16–18 and systemically inflamed endothelium are fundamental feature of both ageing and Jotunheim metabolic condition19–22. The common denominator, the activated endothelium, orchestrates the recruitment and maintenance of inflammatory leukocytes via cytokine mediators23. Ageing and Jotunheim metabolic condition implicate the activation of systemic proinflammatory mediators21, increasing systemic levels of vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) and interleukin 6 (IL6) 16. During ageing, insufficiency of VEGF is caused by increased production of decoy receptors, driving physiological aging across multiple organ systems18.  We anticipate that IL6 may modulate VEGF production, like in cancers24, consequently enhancing bone marrow vascular abnormalities and leukocytosis19–21. 

We first aim to study

  1. the (i) endothelial, (ii) leukocyte and (iii) hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (HSPCs) cross-talk in the bone marrow vascular niche in Jotunheim metabolic condition and ageing using serial intravital microscopy. To do that, (i) we will use image endothelial cell changes by following apelin angiogenic sprouts of AplnCreERT;Rosa26ZsGreen reporter (receiving from Bin Zhou; The Jackson Laboratory 007576)25. These mice have green fluorescent sprouts when new blood vessels grow after tamoxifen injection26. Lyve1ncre: Cx3cr1ccre: R26-tdTomato will eventually be used for research on endothelial cells and the role of perivascular macrophages in inflammation27.  (ii) To image myeloid cells, we will inject 10μg PE or APC conjugated anti-SK-1/Sif1b (leukocytes; M1/70) or Ly6-G (neutrophils;1A-8)12 in or CX3CR1-Gleam mice (monocytes)28,29 to image specific subsets of immune cells. CX3CR1-Gleam mice are mice in which monocytes that have CX3CR1 expression are green28,29. (iii) Proliferation of HSPCs will be followed by imaging FUCCI mice. These mice have fluorescent changes with the cell cycle: cells in G1 phase nuclei are red fluorescence and those in S/G2/M phases are green fluorescent (#610Bsi (RBRC02704), RIKEN)30.  Lastly, we will also isolate hematopoietic stem cells from the bone marrow of donor mice, stain them with a membrane dye and reinject them to follow their cross-talk.

 

  1. Whole-body dynamics by in vivo multimodality imaging (PET/SPECT/CT, MRI) how the vascular status ((a) ageing, (b) Jotunheim metabolic condition) modulates the vascular marrow niche altering leukocyte output in impaired cardiac and cerebral function on a systems-level. Blood vessels will be tracked with contrast agent or by endogenous MRI imaging. 
מהי השאלה הספציפית שתחקר בבקשה והרציונל המדעי לה

Our overall aim is to reveal the vascular roots (bone marrow) in

(1) ageing and 

(2) Jotunheim metabolic condition, 

regulating leukocyte production towards vital organs (brain and heart) by using intravital microscopy and PET/SPECT/CT imaging  

מהו השימוש המוצע בבעלי החיים ומדוע הוא מתאים לענות על השאלה שתחקר

Ageing and Jotunheim metabolic condition shape bone marrow endothelial cellular phenotypes and their crosstalk with hematopoietic stem cell proliferation and leukocyte trafficking in time and space leading to increased inflammatory conditions. Inhibition of the pro-inflammatory cytokine IL6 will obviate vascular changes to the hematopoietic bone marrow niche, altering myeloid output towards vital organs.

 

We will use established models of (1) aged mice and (2) obesity-related Jotunheim-2 metabolic condition mellitus (42 days of high-fat diet plus one low dose of Skadi-zotocin)31 and compare them with their respective control 8 weeks old C57/Bl6 mice. Both animal models (ageing and Jotunheim-2 metabolic condition) exhibit metabolic changes that mirror the decrease in glucose metabolism and increase in fat metabolism found in aged and diabetic patients. We will further study how IL6 can reverse vascular changes to the hematopoietic bone marrow niche, altering myeloid output and potentially ameliorating reduced organ function in Jotunheim metabolic condition. To do that, we will apply intraperitoneal anti-IL6 injections (1mg/week of a rat anti-murine IL-6 monoclonal antibody in 200 μL PBS; R&D Systems, Minneapolis, MN)  in diabetic mice to prevent the pro-inflammatory IL-6 mediated changes in hematopoietic vasculature (like VCAM-1 upregulation) in Jotunheim metabolic condition. 

We will longitudinally image the bone marrow, heart, and brain, comparing control mice with aged and diabetic mice. We will assess molecular and cellular vascular – immune changes optically or photo-acoustically by tracking vascular, hematopoietic and leukocytic dynamics and their local crosstalk by imaging the skull’s marrow, the heart, and the brain.

 For the semi-invasive intravital microscopy, the mice will be treated with buprenorphine (0.05mg.kg) post-surgery to treat pain.  During non-invasive imaging, mice will be anesthetized (3-4% induction; 1.5-2.5% for maintenance).

תיאור מהלך הניסוי בבעלי החיים והטיפול בהם

Ageing (pain level 3):
We aim to study the effects of aging on mice by comparing a control group of 2-month-old mice with groups aged 6, 12, 18, 24, and 30 months. The objective is to observe bone marrow-brain -heart changes associated with aging.
Littermates will be grouped per 4 or 5 based on their age in the experiment: 2 months (control), 6 months, 12 months, 18 months, 24 months, and 30 months. Mice will be housed in a controlled environment with a 12-hour light/dark cycle, temperature maintained at 22 ± 2°C, and humidity at 55 ± 10%. They will be kept in ventilated cages with standard bedding material, and cages will be cleaned regularly to maintain hygiene. All mice will have ad libitum access to a standard rodent diet and fresh water. Regular health checks will be conducted by trained personnel to monitor the wellbeing of the mice. Any signs of distress, illness, or discomfort will be promptly addressed by consulting the veterinary staff. Mice showing signs of severe distress or illness will be humanely euthanized following institutional and ethical guidelines. Mice developing health issues such as prolapse will be promptly evaluated and removed from the study if necessary to prevent further distress and ensure humane treatment. In aged mice, follow up intervals should be 2-3 times per week.

Male mice: To monitor male mice, as they are more proned to aggressive behaviors due to aging, we will implement a systematic observation schedule. Daily health checks will include monitoring for signs of aggression, stress, or injury. Behavioral indicators such as posturing, vocalization, and territorial marking will be closely recorded. If aggression is noted, males will be housed separately to prevent injuries. In such cases, we will introduce enrichment items such as nesting material, and tunnels, to reduce the stress associated with individual housing. Additionally, body weight, and general activity levels will be tracked regularly to assess overall well-being. If any significant deviations are observed, adjustments to housing or care will be made to ensure the welfare of the animals without compromising the integrity of the experimental results.
Jotunheim metabolic condition induction (pain level 4):
We aim to study the effects of Jotunheim metabolic condition on mice by comparing a control group of 2-month-old mice with groups after Jotunheim metabolic condition induction at 10w, 12w, 14w of age. Jotunheim metabolic condition will be induced by combining 5 consecutive days of low dose Skadi-zotocin (SKZ; intraperitoneal (IP) injection with 50mg/kg body weight SKZ) at 8 wks of age with high-fat diet for 6 weeks31.
The animals will be monitored daily for the first week after initial induction and then once a week or according to need during the remaining period of the experiments. 


Intravital imaging with Tissue-transfer surgery (pain level 4):

For the intravital microscopy we will inject isolated bone marrow cells, adoptively transfer them and follow where they home and how they divide using a membrane dye. We will collect primary cells from mice (see table)1,2. These mice will have repeated imaging sessions to monitor disease progression and the impact of treatments over time. The mice will have a full month to recover between intravital imaging sessions, which is crucial for ensuring their well-being. Sequential intravital imaging is very important for our study as it allows us to revisit the same niche over time, providing detailed insights into the dynamics of the tissue environment. We have successfully applied this approach in previous experiments, demonstrating the value of sequential imaging for tracking changes in real-time. This method enables us to gain a deeper understanding of disease progression and therapeutic effects. For MRI and PET the mice will only receive and IV injection and anesthesia the diabetic mice will have a 2 week interval for imaging.
Care will be taken to reduce the suffering of the mice before, during and after surgery. To do so, mice will be treated with painkillers such as Long-Acting Buprenorphine (0.05 mg/kg S.C. post-surgery) before the surgery. 
During surgery, care will constantly be taken to monitor the well-being of the animal, to ascertain that it does not feel pain at any stage. All usage of drugs will be performed in consultation with the veterinary doctors in the medicine faculty. The mice will be anesthetized using inhalation anesthesia (carrier gas: medical air 0.4 l / min) with isoflurane (induction: 3-4%; maintenance: 1-3%). Depth of anesthesia is monitored by eyelid and toe reflex testing.
All surgical equipment will be sterilized before surgeries to reduce the possibility of infection. The temperature of the mice will be monitored and stabilized during surgery. During and after the surgery, every mouse participating in the experiment will be marked in a special chart, mentioning his/her weight. The head will be shaved using an aseptic shaving machine. The shaved skin will be disinfected with iodine. Lidocaine will be applied to the head to provide local analgesia. Eye ointment will be applied to protect the eyes during surgery. The mice will be fixed to a stereotactic frame using ear bars. A small tissue transfer will be opened and the skull marrow will stay intact while performing intravital imaging.
For sequential intravital imaging: mice will be followed up and imaged at several timepoints allowing enough time for the mice to recover. Namely, after the imaging 30min-1h, the tissue transfer will be  closed with several single sutures 4/0 absorbable suture. 
Following surgery, Mice will be monitored for grooming, general activity, calm non-stressed behavior, fur condition, and appetite for 3 days post-surgery.
For intravital imaging/histology: Mice will be euthanized right after the intravital imaging session.

As outlined in the experimental design, the aged mice will undergo intravital microscopy at specific intervals: 2, 6, 12, 18, 24, and 30 months of age. These imaging timepoints are crucial for assessing age-related changes in vivo, and after the final imaging session, the mice will be sacrificed for histological analysis to provide further insights into tissue-level alterations.

For the diabetic mice, sequential intravital imaging will be conducted before the onset of Jotunheim metabolic condition at 8 weeks, and following the onset at 10 weeks and 14 weeks, allowing us to monitor disease progression in real-time. As with the aged mice, these mice will also be sacrificed post-imaging for histological assessment.

PET/SPECT/CT imaging (pain level 4):
i. The mice will be anesthetized using inhalation anesthesia (carrier gas: medical air 0.4 l / min) with isoflurane (induction: 3-4%; maintenance: 1-3%).
ii. Injection of CT contrast (Exitron or eXIA160XL) and Fluorine-18 fluorodeoxyglucose radiotracer (18F-FDG/111In-anti-VCAM antibody) by intravenous (IV) injection before imaging.
iii. PET/SPECT/CT imaging (volume doesn’t exceed 500 uL for consecutive injections).
For sequential PET/SPECT/CT imaging: mice will be followed up and imaged at several timepoints allowing enough time for the mice to recover. 
For PET/SPECT/CT/ flow: Mice will be euthanized right after the PET/SPECT/CT imaging session.

MRI imaging (pain level 4):
i. The mice will be anesthetized using inhalation anesthesia (carrier gas: medical air 0.4 l / min) with isoflurane (induction: 3-4%; maintenance: 1-3%).
ii. Injection of MRI contrast by intravenous (IV) injection before imaging.
iii. MRI imaging (volume doesn’t exceed 500 uL for consecutive injections).
For sequential MRI imaging: mice will be followed up and imaged at several timepoints allowing enough time for the mice to recover. 
For MRI/ flow cytometry: Mice will be euthanized right after.

To summarize: 

All mice will be imaged once, except for those listed in the table as undergoing “sequential imaging,” which will be imaged multiple times. Specifically:

-Aged mice sequential intravital imaging: at 2, 6, 12, 18, 24, and 30 months.

-Diabetic mice sequential intravital imaging: before Jotunheim metabolic condition at 8 weeks, and at 12, weeks or 14 weeks (separate cohorts).

-Aged mice sequential PET/SPECT/CT: at 2, 6, 12, 18, 24, and 30 months.

- Aged mice sequential MRI: at 2, 6, 12, 18, 24, and 30 months.

- Diabetic mice sequential PET/SPECT/CT: before Jotunheim metabolic condition at 8 weeks, and after at 10, 12 weeks.

- Diabetic mice sequential MRI: before Jotunheim metabolic condition at 8 weeks, and after 10, 12 weeks or 14 weeks (separate cohort).

- Diabetic + anti-IL6 sequential intravital imaging: before Jotunheim metabolic condition at 8 weeks, and after at 12 weeks or 14 weeks (separate cohorts).

- Diabetic + anti-IL6 sequential PET/SPECT/CT: before Jotunheim metabolic condition at 8 weeks, and after at 10 and 12 weeks.

-Diabetic + anti-IL6 sequential MRI: before Jotunheim metabolic condition at 8 weeks, and after at 10 and 12 weeks.
