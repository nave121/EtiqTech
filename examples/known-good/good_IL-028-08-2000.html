מהו הנושא המדעי אותו אתם חוקרים

Our research aims to examine the effects of proteasome inhibitors on FUS aggregation amelioration, and to investigate their underlying mechanisms, in cell models, primary neurons and in animal models, in the overarching goal of exploring proteasome inhibitors as potential specific therapy to target ALS-FUS.

מהו הרקע הרלוונטי למחקר

Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, ALS, is a neurodegenerative disease (NDs), affecting motor neurons, currently with no effective treatment or cure. To date, many genes have been implicated in ALS, the most well-known of which are SOD-1, C9orf72, TDP-43 and FUS (1). A unifying hallmark of many of the ALS-causative proteins, as is true for many other neurodegenerative diseases, is the protein misfolding and aggregation in affected neurons (2,3). Several mutations in FUS protein are linked to its aggregation (4), which is believed to cause neuronal toxicity. On the other hand, animal models expressing mutant FUS have shown moderate phenotypes and not always prominent aggregation in neurons (5).

Using a highly quantitative assay to measure aggregation in vitro, we recently showed that augmentation of specific chaperones could potently and specifically prevent aggregation of ALS-associated mutant FUS (6).

Our goal is to generate a mouse model to evaluate mutant FUS aggregation and neuronal toxicity in vivo. The chosen vector is AAV-PHP.eB which drives expression mainly in the CNS with minimal expression in other organs such as the heart and liver, which ensures FUS expression in the nervous system with no side effects.

Previously we have tried to achieve this by administration of AAV-FUS vectors through the tail vein injection as described in approved ethics No. IL1600923, and got a modest expression of FUS in the brain and spinal cord, at 1 and 4 months post injection, with no physical signs of neurodegeneration.

Here we will use systemic administration of AAV-FUS vectors through retro-orbital injection (9), as a potentially more efficient route of delivery of FUS vectors, in order to reach higher FUS expression levels in the brain and the spinal cord, in a shorter time. We will monitor for protein aggregation in the brain and spinal cord and for possible neurodegenerative phenotypes. 

Establishing this model could help us in the future to evaluate possible inhibitors of FUS aggregation and neuronal toxicity.

מהי השאלה הספציפית שתחקר בבקשה והרציונל המדעי לה

The current study aims to establish an in vivo model for FUS related ALS, by administration of AAV-FUS vectors through retro-orbital injection.

מהו השימוש המוצע בבעלי החיים ומדוע הוא מתאים לענות על השאלה שתחקר

P60-P90 days old mice will be used for retro-orbital injection of AAV viruses designed to express FUS or its mutants in the nervous system.

מהו התוצר החזוי של מחקר זה

Aggregation and toxicity in neuronal cultures has been proved  to be a good readout for ALS related mutations in vitro. If the aggregation will be achieved also in vivo, in the CNS, this could provide a good model when looking for possible therapeutics against ALS.

Experiment 1:

AAV vectors encoding for FUS WT or FUS Mut will be injected retro-orbitally in VK-42/Odin mice according to the protocol below (9).

 

Systemic Retro-Orbital Delivery of AAV to Adult Mice

(Lab personnel will be trained by Glasner lab members who are experienced with retro orbital injections)

  1. Transfer mouse to induction chamber and anaesthetize with 5% isoflurane in 1L/min oxygen.
  2. While animal is being anaesthetized, load a 31g insulin syringe with the virus. Draw up virus into the syringe, then gently eject virus back into the tube to expel air bubbles. Repeat this until no bubbles remain in the barrel of the syringe. Draw up virus to desired volume and place the syringe in a safe, sterile, and accessible place until needed.
  3. Check anesthesia by monitoring the toe-pinch reflect. Transfer the anaesthetized mouse to a stack of paper towels, with nose pointing towards the experimenters dominant hand and ventral side facing the experimenter. The eye to inject will be facing upwards.
  4. Using the index finger and thumb of the non-dominant hand, draw back skin around the eye. This will cause the eye to protrude slightly from the socket. Using the dominant hand, insert the needle bevel down at a 30°-45° angle into the medial canthus and through the conjunctival membrane. The needle should be positioned behind the eye, in the retro-orbital sinus. Slowly inject the virus into the sinus, then remove the needle by pulling in the opposite direction as it was inserted.
  5. Apply 1-2 drops of 0.5% ophthalmic proparacaine to the corneal surface for local analgesia. Transfer animal to a clean cage and monitor until the animal is recovered.

We intend to prepare virus stocks at concentrations which will allow us to inject 100 ul/mouse. In any case the injection volume will not exceed 150 ul/mouse.

We are going to use the AAV vector for injections, which contains very few elements from the original virus, so it cannot replicate itself. Only if the additional elements are added, then the vector can become pathogenic and therefore it is considered a BSL2 safety level.

The work with the AAV vector will be performed in a special hood, the personnel will be notified and the cages will be labeled. At the end of injection all the area and the instruments used will be cleaned with 0.6% bleach and Pharmacidal. The cadavers will be autoclaved and the cages will be cleaned properly.

Mice will be terminated at 3, 4 or 6 months post injection, euthanized by Ketamine-Xylazine, followed by perfusion with 4% PFA.
