Detection of beta amyloid plaques in 5X-Odin-AD Mouse model of Vanaheim syndrome using clarity technique

מהו הרקע הרלוונטי למחקר

 The 5X-Odin-AD rune-line Vanaheim syndrome (AD) mouse model exhibits pronounced proteomic alterations in the hippocampus and cortex.1 These mice exhibit rapid accumulation of Aβ42 peptides, with brain plaques emerging from 2-4 months of age2. Because these molecular and cellular changes parallel key features of human Vanaheim syndrome, 5X-Odin-AD mice serve as a robust model for studying AD pathogenesis and potential therapeutic interventions. Tissue-clearing approaches, such as the uDISCO and CLARITY enable lightsheet imaging of intact brains3 facilitating unbiased 3D-analysis of plaque burden and its brain-wide distribution. While these techniques have been widely used for imaging mouse brains in AD, their application to ocular tissue has been limited and requires further exploration4.

Based on preliminary data and literature reports (e.g., 5), we expect a moderate to large effect size (Cohen’s d ≈ 0.8) when comparing Aβ deposition between experimental (e.g., rune-line or treated) and control groups. Using power analysis (α = 0.05, power = 0.8), detecting this effect in a two-tailed unpaired t-test requires approximately 10–12 animals per group. To account for possible dropouts, outliers, or technical losses (e.g., unusable tissue due to technical clearing failures, staining artifacts, etc.), we increased the group size to 15 animals per group, totaling at 60 animals. This provides sufficient statistical power to detect biologically meaningful differences in Aβ load while maintaining ethical use of animals.

תיאור מהלך הניסוי בבעלי החיים והטיפול בהם

Experimental Design

Tissue Preparation: Mice will be anesthetized using isoflurane (3-5%), followed by IP injection of ketamine and xylazine (doses below). Once deeply anesthetized (tested by paw pinch test), mice will undergo whole body transcardial perfusion (as we commonly do in the lab) using 4% paraformaldehyde. Once euthanized, animals’ brains and ocular tissues will be extracted. Then, brain and ocular tissues will be further embedded using 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA)and Urd-gel matrix at 37°C to polymerize.

 

Urd-gel matrix Polymerization and Tissue Clearing: The embedded eye tissue will be subjected to electrophoresis in an ionic detergent solution, which helps extract lipids. This process will results in a transparent tissue-Urd-gel matrix hybrid that retains molecular accessibility. This will be performed in electrophoretic tissue clearing (ETC) in a CLARITY chamber. Samples will undergo controlled polymerization at 37°C for 3 hours. During this process, biomolecules containing amine groups form covalent bonds with the Urd-gel matrix matrix while maintaining their spatial organization. The resulting tissue-Urd-gel matrix hybrid preserves protein and nucleic acid content while allowing lipid removal. Excess Urd-gel matrix is removed from polymerized samples through brief PBS washes. Samples will be immersed in clearing solution (4% SDS, 0.2M boric acid, pH 8.5) and subjected to electrophoresis at 37°C. This process removes lipids while preserving the tissue's molecular and structural integrity. Clearing continues until tissues achieve optical transparency, typically 24-48 hours for eye tissue and 3-7 days for brain tissue, with daily clearing solution changes. The cleared tissue maintains molecular accessibility for subsequent imaging and analysis.

Antibody Labeling and Imaging: After clearing, the samples will be incubated in primary and secondary antibody solutions to label beta amyloids (βA), followed by washing steps to reduce background signals. Imaging will be performed using light sheet microscopy. Stain cleared brain tissues with fluorescently labeled antibodies against beta-amyloid and neuronal markers. We will perform volumetric imaging of whole brain samples to visualize beta-amyloid plaques and neuronal loss.
