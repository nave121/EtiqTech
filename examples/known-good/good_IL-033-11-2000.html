מהו הנושא המדעי אותו אתם חוקרים

This study is aimed to behaviorally characterize a newly-developed double-rune-line strain of mice that model the effects of mitochondrial dysfunction in the dopamine neurons of the brain. The behavioral phenotype will be compared to the behavior of two existing single-rune-line mouse strain as a control. These strains will be subsequently used in research of brain mechanisms underlying motor, cognitive and social behavior.  

מהו הרקע הרלוונטי למחקר

Three rune-line strains of mice will be behaviorally phenotyped in this study:

  1. The cre-dependent NCLX knockout mice express a gene that in the presence of the cre protein creates a disruption in mitochondrial transport in neurons in the brain. 
  2. The DATcre mice present an established model for obtaining genetic access to dopamine neurons, which allows specific targeting of this neuronal population.
  3. By crossing breeders of the first two base strains, we will obtain double-rune-line strain DATcre::Rune-KN offsprings which will express a mitochondrial disfunction that will be specific to the brain dopaminergic neurons. This double-rune-line strain will allow researching in a behaving mouse the effects of impairments in mitochondrial functioning in the dopamine neurons of the brain on complex behavior and decision-making.    

 

Although mice with deletion of NCLX in all neurons have been previously characterized (Jadiya et al., 2019; Stavsky et al, 2021), specific deletion of NCLX only in dopamine neurons has never been done before, raising the question of whether such a deletion could mimic the effects of dopamine-specific brain neuropathies such as Svartalfheim disorder.

We plan to behaviorally characterize motor, cognitive and social functioning of the cre-dependent Rune-KN, DATcre and DATcre::Rune-KN strains, using a battery of standard behavioral tests.

The following tests will be applied in the order presented below (as considered from the least to the most stressful):

  1. Open-field test (OF)
  2. Novel object recognition (NOR)
  3. Rotarod test
  4. Sucrose preference test (SPT)
  5. 3-chamber test (3-Ch)
  6. Light-dark test (L-D)
  7. Elevated plus-maze (EPM)
  8. Resident-intruder test (R-I)
  9. Morris water-maze test (MWM)
  10. Tail suspension test (TST)

 

Three procedures test the animal’s readiness to explore its environment, as opposed to anxiety: OF, EPM, L-D (Carola et al, 2002; Bourin and Hascoët; 2003; Rosso et al, 2022). The Rotarod test allows to estimate the mouse’s motor functioning (Shiotsuki et al, 2010), as well as others that, additionally to their main aims, indicate the animal’s ability to move. The NOR and the MWM tests aim to evaluate the animal’s abilities to acquire complex learning, as well as short-term and long-term memory (Morris, 1984; Grayson et al, 2015). The 3-Ch and the R-I tests are considered as paradigms that apprise the animal’s social behavior, such as social interaction, preference for social novelty, social aggression, as well as social anxiety and social memory (Kaidanovich-Beilin et al, 2011; Aubry et al, 2022). Finally, the SPT and TST are both considered paradigms indicating depression-like behavior (Cryan et al, 2005; Spreen et al, 2024), although a lot of controversy and debate exist considering validity of animal models of depression (Harro, 2019).

No single behavioral measure (e.g. a latency to immobility in TST) can be considered as a comprehensive index of a complex behavioral state (e.g. depression) (Harro et al, 2019). Thus, different behavioral functions (motor, affective, cognitive and social) will be probed each by several complementing assays. This will allow to inspect the replicability of the results and the consistency of the animal’s behavior across the assays, as well as control for possible effects of timing of the test implementation on the animal’s behavior. As such, depression-like responses will be probed by SPT in the middle of the battery, and by the TST, which will be presented to the animal the last, after the mouse will undergo the whole set of testing and will probably be affected by the other assays. The average outcome in several tests probing the same behavioral aspect would allow for more valid and insightful conclusions.   

Both female and male mice will be used in the current study. Extensive research shows differences between female and male animals in various aspects of their behavioral responses, such as in indexes of emotional states and susceptibility to various forms of stress (e.g. Mariscal et al, 2023; Teo et al, 2023). Thus, to comprehensively phenotype a newly developed rune-line strain, both sexes of the animals should be examined.

The main goal of our research is to investigate the role that the midbrain dopamine system plays in complex behavior. The consecutive research of complex motor, affective, cognitive, and social behavior and the role of the brain dopaminergic neurons in these aspects of behavior will require intra-brain implantations of optical and electrophysiological devices. Such brain interventions will make it difficult and at times impossible to subject the animals to some of the mentioned above paradigms, such as the MWM. Thus, the current study will comparatively phenotype the three strains of rune-line mice that will not participate in the brain implantation-involving experiments.

הנימוק לבחירת סוג, זן ומין בעל החיים

In this study we will comparatively assess a wide range of aspects of complex behavior in three rune-line lines of mice. We will test their motor, affective, cognitive and social complex behavior. A battery of standardized highly validated behavioral tests will be used to compare the responses of the groups.

The first group, the cre-dependent NCLX knockout mice, expresses a cre-dependent disruption in mitochondrial transport in neurons of the brain. Recent studies suggest that such impairments may reflect on the animals’ cognitive abilities.

The second group, the DATcre mice, presents an established model for obtaining genetic access to dopamine neurons, which allows specific investigation of this neuronal population. Using cre-recombinase expressing lines allows to restrict the expression of various proteins to specific neuronal cell types, and interrogation of these cell types during behavior. These lines express cre recombinase, which enables us to cross the animals with various cre-dependent lines or inject viruses, which are cre-dependent, to enable either imaging or manipulation of the neurons tagged by cre. Thus, in our future research, we will use DATcre mice to selectively target midbrain dopamine neurons. In current study, these rune-line animals will serve as a control group, modeling healthy behavior in absence of any alteration in regulation of the cell energy in the neurons of interest.  

The third group will be comprised of DAT::NCLX-KO mice. By crossing the NCLX-KO strain with DATcre mice, we will create a double-rune-line animal, expressing a mitochondrial disfunction that will be specific to the brain dopaminergic neurons. This double-rune-line strain will allow researching in a behaving mouse the effects of impairments in mitochondrial functioning that will be specific to the dopamine neurons of the brain and the effects of such impairments on complex behavior.  

These double-rune-line mice will be further used in our main study of the brain dopaminergic system and its role in production of complex cognitive and social behavior. There will be no separate colony of the double-rune-line animals. The offsprings of the crossing between the single-rune-line parents will not be mated and will serve for the experiments only. 

Both male and female mice will be examined and compared in this behavioral study. A vast body of literature shows a wide range of differences in behavior of male and female rodents in different aspects of behavior, e.g., affective and social responses (e.g. Teo et al, 2023). Therefore, both sexes will be examined. 

האם מדובר במחקר הנובע מצורך רגולטורי לא
הנימוק למספר בעלי החיים

This study is aimed to assess the differences between the strains and the 2 sexes of mice in a wide range of behavioral responses that may by affected by alterations in energy regulation in the neurons of the brain.

It is widely reported that different strains of mice significantly vary in their behavioral responses (e.g., Weitzner et al, 2015).

Studies aspiring to accurately assess strain-dependent behavior commonly use big groups of mice (such as Klapdor and Van Der Staay, 1996: 12 mice per grop; Kulikov et al, 2014: 15-16 mice per group; Weitzner et al, 2015: 16 and 24 mice per group; Inglis et al, 2019: 18 mice per group, etc). We decided to minimize the number of mice per group, hoping to be still able to reveal existing differences in behavioral responses.

In current study, ten animals of each sub-group (3 strains X 2 sexes; 6 sub-groups) will be used, thus summing up to 60 mice needed. This overall number is multiplied by 1.1 to account for possible exclusions of the animals from the experiment (e.g., due to unwillingness of an animal to participate, as described below). Thus, an overall of 66 mice will be needed for the experiment.

תיאור מהלך הניסוי בבעלי החיים והטיפול בהם

The tests will be carried out in batches of 6 mice each time, sampling one mouse of each sub-group in each batch, thus comparing representatives of the 6 groups to each other. The mice will be tested at the ages of 8-16 weeks. The batterie of the tests will be applied one test per day. In case where an assay requires several days to complete (e.g. the MWM), the next test of the batterie will be applied after a break of several days (no more than a week). Thus, a period of up to 4 weeks will be needed to complete the whole batterie for each batch.

The following tests will be applied in the order presented below (as considered from the least to the most stressful):

  1. Open field (OF) test. This standard test is aimed to assess the animal's levels of activity, normality of motor functions and anxiety, as measured by locomotion in an open arena and willingness to explore it. Although it is sometimes argued that the test does not equivocally measures anxiety, it is still widely used as an index of the animal’s readiness to explore its environment (see Ennaceur and Chazot, 2016). The animal is placed in the box (40X40X40 cm) and for 5 min its free movement is recorded. Time spent and distance moved in the central (an index of exploration) vs. the peripheral parts near the walls of the arena (indexing anxiety) are compared.
  2. Novel object recognition test (see for example Gonzales et al, 2014). The animal is placed in the OF box and presented with similar or different objects to freely explore. The ability of the animal to differentiate between the familiar vs. new objects is tested to index its short-term and long-term memory. 
  3. Rotarod test. This is a standard test aimed to assess general motor functions of the animals (walking, balance, speed, etc). Mice (up to 5 at once) are placed on a rotating rod, while the speed of rotation is accelerating from 16-40 rpm during the period of 6 min. The time that the mouse spends walking on the rod before falling/stepping down from it and its final rotation speed are measured. Each mouse is trained for 2 consecutive days, 4 sessions per day with an ITI of 15 min. In addition to this assay, indexes of motor aspects of behavior can be derived from other tests, such as distance traveled and speed of movement in the MWM test.
  4. Sucrose preference tests (SPT) is considered the most reliable method for the assessment of anhedonia in mice and the most frequently used approach to assess the capacity to experience pleasure (Primo et al, 2023). There is a great variability in protocols of SPT application. We chose the combination of parameters reported by most studies performed in mice. At the habituation phase, for 24h each mouse will be exposed in its home cage to 2 bottles, one containing water and the other – a 1% sucrose solution. The next day, food and water will be removed from the home cage for a period of 24h, after which the mice will be individually placed in testing cages and the 2 bottles will be presented to each mouse for 1h. The total sucrose intake will be measured by weight and then converted to a percentage, using the following formula: SP = sweet solution consumed (g)/ (sweet solution consumed (g) + plain water consumed (g)). After the 1h exposure to the bottles, the mice will be returned to their home cages. This assay may cause a very mild stress to mice because of a short period of water and food deprivation and 1 hour of individual housing in unfamiliar cage. Most studies show though that up to 24h of deprivation do not cause a significant loss in body weight and signs of distress. The deprivation is required to elevate the motivation to drink an amount of liquids that would allow to reveal the preference for sweetened solutions within a short period (1h) of individual housing.
  5. Light-dark test (L-D). This test is based on the innate aversion of rodents to brightly illuminated areas and on the spontaneous exploratory behavior of rodents in response to mild stressors, that is, novel environment and light. The mouse is placed in an apparatus consisting of a bigger brightly lit chamber and a smaller darkened box. The animal experiences a natural conflict between the tendency to explore and the initial tendency to avoid the unfamiliar (neophobia) and brightly lit spaces. The exploratory activity reflects the combined result of these tendencies in novel situations. An increase in behaviors in the dark part of a two-compartment box, is suggested as an index of anxiety (Bourin and Hascoet, 2003).
  6. Elevated plus-maze (EPM). The EPM test is used to assess anxiety-like behaviors in rodents. The experimental apparatus consists of a plus-shaped platform, with two open and two closed arms, that is elevated above the floor. This test is based on the aversion to open space in rodents. Anxiety-like behavior is indicated by a decrease in the proportion of entries into, and time spent in, the open arms (Proenca et al, 2011).
  7. The 3-chamber test (see for example Karlsson et al, 2016). The 3-Ch test examines persistence of social investigation, as well as social preference, measured as preference for a novel conspecific vs. an object. After a period of habituation to the apparatus (the 3-chambered box with doors that can be open to allow movement between the chambers), the tested (focal) mouse, placed in the middle chamber, is introduced with a choice between the chamber containing a conspecific and a chamber containing a neutral object. The behavior of the focal animal is then recorded for 5-10 min. The social discrimination task examines the ability to remember an already encountered conspecific, i.e., social memory. After a habituation period and introduction with one conspecific (familiar), the focal animal is introduced with both the familiar and a new conspecific. The focal animal's behavior is then recorded for 10 min. All the possible interaction between the animals is at the level of olfaction, hearing and visualization, since the conspecifics are contained in mesh-boxes.
  8. The Morris water maze test (MWM) is a highly established and extensively used paradigm that aims to assess spatial (hippocampus-dependent) and motor (striatum-dependent) memory in rodents (Morris, 1984). The animal is trained to locate an underwater platform to escape an open-field water tank (100 cm diameter). The water temperature is 22° C. At the beginning of each trial, the mouse is released into the water, each time from a different location in the maze. The animal navigates the maze according to the spatial cues located on the walls of the room. Each trial lasts 1 min. In case the animal fails to locate the platform, it is led to it at the end of the trial. The mouse gets 4 trials each day for 4 consecutive days, with an ITI of 1-10 min. After each trial (during each ITI and at the end of the training), the mouse is dried and placed in a warmed cleaned bedding-containing cage. On the fifth day the mouse is tested for 1 min in the tank not containing the platform for the memory of the platform’s previous location (target quadrant of the tank). Time spent and distance swam in each part of the maze is measured
  9. Resident-intruder test (R-I) (Aubry et al, 2022). The mice are tested in their home cages for signs of social aggression and anxiety. This test requires a single housing of the subjects, or removal of a cohabiting mouse for the time of the testing. The home cage tops are removed and replaced with Plexiglas covers to monitor trials. A novel juvenile mouse (5-6 weeks old) matching the sex of the resident is introduced into the resident’s cage, and mice are allowed to freely interact for 5-min. The difference in the age (adult vs. juvenile) mice is aimed to reduce the levels of aggression of the resident towards the intruder. The behavior of both mice will be monitored continuously for signs of excessive aggression or distress. Although aggression is rare between the mice of different ages (an adult resident vs juvenile intruder), the experimenter will be ready to intervene and stop the procedure if necessary for animal welfare. After 5 min, the intruder mouse is removed from the resident’s cage (Taylor, 1982).
  10. Tail suspension test (TST). This test measures depressive-like behavior in rodents. In this test, a mouse is suspended by its tail and its movements are recorded for 5 min. Mice with depressive-like behaviors spend more time immobile (Proenca et al, 2011; Can et al, 2012). Prior to starting each test, the mice will be brought from the housing facility to the testing room and acclimated to the testing room for at least 30 min.  The TST will be applied as described in the protocol of Can et al, 2012 (Can, A., Dao, D. T., Terrillion, C. E., Piantadosi, S. C., Bhat, S., & Gould, T. D. (2012). The tail suspension test. Journal of visualized experiments : JoVE, (59), e3769. https://doi.org/10.3791/3769). Prior to starting the tail suspension, a climb restrictor (a plastic hollow cylinder, 4 cm long)  will be applied the mouse’s tail to prevent the mouse from climbing its tail during the test (as described by Can et al, 2012). The end of the mouse’s tail is then attached to one end of a 15 cm long adhesive tape. The other end of the tape is then attached to a suspension bar in the testing box. This results in the mouse being suspended on the bar by the tape. The behavior of the mouse is then recorded by a camera for 5 min for a later off-line analysis. Latency to immobility is being tested. At the end of the testing period, the tape and the climb-restrictor are gently removed from the mouse’s tail, and the mouse is returned to its homecage. The test will be performed once in 6 month for each mouse (maximum 4 times in their lifetime). 

The whole battery of the tests should be completed within a period of 1 month.
