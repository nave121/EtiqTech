# Introduction (from the original form)

In 1994, the Knesset of Israel enacted a law dealing with the regulation of experiments on
animals. The Ministry of Health is charged with the implementation of this law through the
Council for Experiments on Animals.
No experiments on animals shall be conducted in the State of Israel except in accordance with
the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Law - Experiments on Animals (5754-1994). You are
requested to read the law and confirm this with your signature.
The law states that no permit shall be granted for conducting experiments on an animal if the
purpose of the experiment can be achieved by reasonable alternative means. Please verify
that there are no alternatives to the use of animals in the proposed experiment, and confirm
this with your signature.
If the experiment involves the use of hazardous materials (biological, chemical, or physical),
you must obtain the approval of the Institutional Safety Committee.
You are requested to announce your commitment to conduct the experiments solely in
facilities that have received the approval of the Council for Experiments on Animals.

One must not deviate from the conditions of the permit without explicit written approval from
the Permit Committee.
By your signature on this form, you undertake to act in accordance with the provisions of the
Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Law, the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Rules, and
according to the Council's guidelines.

# Explanatory Notes for the Request Form

# Submitter for Conducting Experiments

Filling out the request form obligates the applicant to perform a thought process according to
the 3R principles for the research plan. This process surfaces critical points in the research
plan that require explanation and additional attention. This is in order to enable the Permit
Committee to discuss and decide whether the request meets the requirements for granting a
permit to conduct an experiment on animals.

Filling out the request form must provide a clear response regarding the implementation of
the principles of working with animals – 3R. The applicant must fill it out in a manner that
explains and justifies the proposed use of animals with attention to the 3Rs.

The request is not like a request for a research grant, and it must be filled out in a manner
suitable for the needs of issuing the permit.

In the event that the Permit Committee finds the request meets the requirements and issues a
permit to conduct the experiment on animals, the permit holder must know that the permit is
personal and is not transferable.


Note: The permit is a document with legal validity and constitutes a license on behalf of the
State of Israel to perform the experiment on the animal, please give your full attention to filling
out the request.

Start from the smallest experimental unit (for example from the chosen group size) - explain
its size and why it was chosen, and link it to the experimental question.
Continue to the number of groups (if 5 groups are needed explain why 5 groups and the like),

It is desirable to add statistical reasoning for group size for the required statistical power of
the results.
Link quantitatively the required numbers to the experimental stages. For example, when it is

used in more than one stage).

## Subsection: Severity Level Classification

Classification of severity levels for experiments on animals
Explanation for a non-professional person (professional definitions follow)
Every severity grade assesses the potential for pain and suffering to which the animal is likely
to be exposed, even if it occurs strictly momentarily in the experiment. This does not mean
that the potential will be realized. Note that the potential for pain and suffering and not the
procedure are what determine the severity level. Note that if a certain severity grade was
determined for an experiment, it does not mean that the animal will be exposed to the said
pain and suffering the entire time of the experiment.
**Severity Grade 1:** Collection of organs from animals that did not undergo any experiment and
were euthanized in an accepted manner for the purpose of organ collection.

Severity Grade 2: Research where the animal is not expected to experience pain and suffering
or such where the potential for pain and suffering to which the animal will be exposed is
minimal and does not exceed temporary discomfort.
Example, research where the pain and suffering does not exceed the pain involved in a single
injection (for example taking a blood sample) to a naïve animal.
Severity Grade 3: Research where the potential for pain and suffering to which the animal will


be exposed is at a level that must not cause significant changes in physiological parameters
such as heart rate and respiration or in its social behavior or in the appearance of the animal.
During or after experiments in this category, animals will not show signs of self-injury,
anorexia, dehydration, hyperactivity, lying down or crouching beyond what is accepted,
excessive vocalization, particularly aggressive behavior or phenomena of isolation.
An experiment performed under general anesthesia from which the animal will not wake up
(acute experiment) will be classified as Severity Grade 3 even if an experimental procedure of
a higher severity grade is performed on the animal. This is because the action will be
performed solely when the animal is under general anesthesia from which it will not wake up.
--- Page 8 ---

Severity Grade 4: Research where the potential for pain and suffering to which the animal will
be exposed is such that is accompanied by a change in the natural physiological activity of
the animal, or by prolonged restraint, or development of local cancer. Animals in such
experiments will be treated by means of giving analgesics so that the autonomous function of
the animal will not be impaired, provided that giving analgesics has the ability to relieve the
animal. Under the group of experiments that is likely to fit this severity grade classification
there are types of experiments where giving analgesics is not necessary. Severity Grade 4
classification stems from the essence of the procedure and its effect on the animal and not
from the very act of giving analgesics.
Severity Grade 4 is determined according to the experimental procedure and its effect on the
animal. Severity Grade 4 classification for an experiment determines that the pain caused to
the animal during the experiment will be treated with analgesics according to need (this is in
contrast to grades 3 and 5).
Severity Grade 5: Research where the potential for pain and suffering to which the animal will
be exposed is substantial and continuous. Under this classification will be found studies in
which the animals are not treated with analgesics, studies where widespread cancerous
tumors will develop or studies likely to cause death (such as use of toxins). Scientific
justification must be given why it is not possible to use analgesics.
Experiments are likely to be classified as Severity Grade 5 even if the experimental procedure
is of a lower severity grade. Thus for example an experiment where the procedure done is of
Severity Grade 4 but it is not possible to give analgesics in it, because the very act of giving
analgesics conflicts with the experiment goals. In this case classification of Severity Level 5
indicates the inability to relieve the animal by medicinal means (analgesics), however other
means to relieve the animal will indeed be operated. Another case that raises the severity
classification from level 4 to 5 is research in metastatic cancer. In this case even if analgesics
are given, Severity Grade 5 classification will still be given. The reason for this is the inability to
assess the metastatic nature. So too in toxicity experiments, the classification of Severity
Grade 5 indicates the potential risk, however usually these experiments do not cause death –
since their goal is not to kill animals but to provide a drug in a safe dosage for use in humans.
Few are the studies receiving classification of Severity Grade 5 that express in practice the
conditions of substantial and continuous pain and suffering.
**General Notes**


```
● Research will always be classified at the highest severity grade even if most of the
research stages and even most of the research duration are done at a low severity level.
For example, stage A – breeding, stage B – toxicity.
● Severity grades do not reflect only the potential for pain and suffering that can be
caused due to the experimental procedure but also whether there is treatment with
analgesics. Therefore granting approval at level 4, for example, can be preferable in terms
of prevention of cruelty to animals compared to approval of the same experiment which
would be classified as an experiment of Severity Grade 3.
● In addition to the severity level classification, clear and absolute conditions for stopping
the participation of the animal in the experiment will be determined for every experiment.
These conditions will define specific and relevant indices for the planned experimental
model such that they will allow relieving as much as possible the pain and suffering of the
animal. Removal of the animal from the experiment according to these conditions will be
performed even if the experiment goals were not achieved. I.e., conditions are defined
that if met, there will be no more scientific value in continuing the existence of the
research in the animal and the consideration of prevention of cruelty to animals will
overcome any other consideration.
● An additional step taken for the welfare of animals in studies of Severity Grade
classification 4 and 5, is the existence of a designated monitoring procedure for the
health of the animals in the experiment.
```

Professional Explanation for Researchers
Below is the severity level classification determined by the Council on March 21, 2006, and in
an update from September 17, 2017.
**Grade of pain and suffering during the experiment and after it**

**Severity Grade 1:** Collection of organs from animals that did not undergo any experiment and
were euthanized in an accepted manner for the purpose of organ collection.

Severity Grade 2: Experiments which cause at most mild and temporary discomfort only.
Examples:

1. Injections Oral, SC, IP, IM, IV (not including Intrathoracic, Intracardiac, Intradermal), that
    do not cause pain (beyond the injection pain) or harmful phenomena.
2. Capture, observation and/or marking of animals.
3. Behavioral experiments without stress (not including water maze, predator-prey
    experiments and others at a similar level).
4. Causing mild pain that the animal can avoid.
5. Tattooing for marking.
6. Taking a blood sample from peripheral blood vessels (not including heart, retro-orbital
    sinus) up to the approved amount, without anesthesia.
7. Feeding animals a diet that does not cause any clinical phenomenon.

Severity Grade 3: Experiments with potential for causing minor stress, suffering or mild pain


and without causing continuous disturbance in the general state, in wellbeing or in the proper
activity of the animal.
Experiments at this level must not cause significant changes in the appearance of the animal,
in physiological parameters such as heart rate and respiration or in its social behavior. During
or after experiments in this category, animals will not show signs of self-injury, anorexia,
dehydration, hyperactivity, lying down or crouching beyond what is accepted, excessive
vocalization, particularly aggressive behavior or phenomena of isolation.
An experiment performed under general anesthesia from which the animal will not wake up
(acute experiment) will be classified as Severity Grade 3 even if an experimental procedure of
a higher severity grade is performed on the animal. This is because the action will be
performed solely when the animal is under general anesthesia from which it will not wake up.

Examples:

1. Surgeries performed under general anesthesia and the animal is euthanized before its
    return to consciousness.
2. Cannulations and insertion of catheters to different body cavities under anesthesia.
3. Small surgeries under anesthesia (such as biopsies, laparoscopies).
4. Taking a blood sample under anesthesia from the heart or from the retro-orbital sinus.

widespread cancerous tumors will develop or studies likely to cause death (such as use of
toxins). Scientific justification must be given why it is not possible to use analgesics.
Experiments are likely to be classified as Severity Grade 5 even if the experimental procedure
is of a lower severity grade. Thus for example an experiment where the procedure done is of
Severity Grade 4 but it is not possible to give analgesics in it, because the very act of giving
analgesics conflicts with the experiment goals. In this case classification of Severity Level 5
indicates the inability to relieve the animal by medicinal means (analgesics), however other
means to relieve the animal will indeed be operated. Another case that raises the severity
classification from level 4 to 5 is research in metastatic cancer. In this case even if analgesics
are given, Severity Grade 5 classification will still be given. The reason for this is the inability to
assess the metastatic nature. So too in toxicity experiments, the classification of Severity
Grade 5 indicates the potential risk, however usually these experiments do not cause death –
since their goal is not to kill animals but to provide a drug in a safe dosage for use in humans.
Few are the studies receiving classification of Severity Grade 5 that express in practice the
conditions of substantial and continuous pain and suffering.
**Note:** In severity grades 4 and 5 and in general, it is not the giving of the analgesic that
defines the severity level but the essence of the experimental procedure and its significance
to the animal. Thus research of Severity Grade 4 is possible where analgesics will not be
given, and research of Severity Grade 5 is possible where analgesics will be given (or another


severity grade than 4).

**New:** Choosing Severity Grade 4 or 5 will open a window to note the monitoring conditions
required for the animal in the experiment. The monitoring conditions will usually be
accompanied by documentation of indices that will be determined and will even be
accompanied if needed by score tables.

subsections).
Determination of conditions of the type, consult with the veterinarian is not an accepted

One must relate to specific indices for the experiment (score, cancerous tumor size, cancer

## Subsection: Previous Experiment on the Animal

The request submitter must state if any of the animals intended to take part in the requested
research already participated in an experiment conducted by virtue of another permit. This is
not a general question but specific. Prevention of cruelty to animals rules 2001 state that
repeated use of an animal for an experiment must be avoided, unless it involves exceptional
circumstances detailed in the rules. The default is that an animal does not participate in more
than one experiment.

## Subsection: Euthanasia

**New:** State the euthanasia method suitable for the animal. Note we moved to noting the
method according to the euthanasia route (route) and not according to the euthanasia agent.
After choosing the euthanasia route one must detail the manner of performing the euthanasia
including choice of agents and dosages. Note that if there is a need to harvest organs not all
agents are suitable.

## Subsection: Fate of Animal

State the suitable fate for the animal at the end of the experiment. Whether it will be
euthanized or rehabilitated.
The Council encourages any possibility to rehabilitate animals at the end of the experiment.
Rehabilitation options are:
Rehabilitation (for example monkeys)


Offer for adoption (for example dogs cats rabbits)
Return to herd (for example farm animals)
Return to nature (for example wild animals)
**Reminder** - if euthanasia is required for the purpose of organ harvesting state this in the
experiment course.
